# Note 8, sequence labeling: its figures and numbers

Planned on 10 Oct 2026 from class 3, slides 16 to 19, and class 4, slides 3 to 8 (four research agents, three drafted plans and a judge, in `course-materials/natural-language-processing/note8/judge.md`, with 23 decisions and 21 differences from the slides), and built without a mockup round: Joaquín asked for the notes to go ahead without his feedback. Its decisions follow the judge's recommendations, with the numbers of this note's own build (the plan's came from note 6's vectors). The build scripts are in `note8/w8/`; `facts8.txt` (`data8.mjs --facts`), `hmm8.out.txt`, `overflow8.out.txt` and `overflow8b.out.txt` there hold every number below. A review from five angles (facts, theory, Spanish, figures, the reader's path), each checked by a skeptical verifier, confirmed 77 findings, all applied (`review8-confirmed.txt`).

## Notation

- The slides' symbols: x, y, y₀, y′, K, i, j, k; P ∈ ℝ^{N×K}, P_{i,k}; A ∈ ℝ^{K×K} plus its start row A_{y₀,k}; s(x, y); p(y_i = k | x), p(y | x), Z(x), α_i(k); h, h₀, P(h_i | h_{i−1}), P(x_i | h_i), P(x, h), c(·), h*, δ_i(k). Added: w_k and the bias folded in with a 1 in front of v_{x_i} (note 6's convention), ℒ = log Z(x) − s(x, y) (Lample et al. 2016), the base cases α₁ and δ₁, the backtrace with argmax and no new letter.
- N for the sentence's length (the notes' settled letter), not the slides' n. Italic P with two indices is the head's table of scores; an upright P followed by parentheses a probability, as notes 4 and 5 wrote them; section 5 says so once, in those words ("P with a bar" read as an overbar). h is the HMM's tags here, said once, not the logistic output of earlier notes; y and h are the same kind of thing. c(·) counts. log is natural, said once. β, π, ψ, θ and T as a letter are not used.
- Label codes (O, B-PER, I-LOC, PART, NOUN) are set in the sans (`.sl-code`), so that I-PER never reads as part I of a chapter label. Two kinds of names, persons PER and places LOC, K = 2 · 2 + 1 = 5; 17 tags for the HMM.
- Terms: A is the transition matrix / la matriz de transiciones, named where it is introduced and in section 3's heading; sequence labeling / etiquetado de secuencias; a labeling / una secuencia de etiquetas («secuencia»), never «etiquetado» for one labeling; head / cabezal; encoder in English; score / puntuación, puntuar; forward recursion / recursión hacia adelante (the English adds "often called the forward algorithm", never "forward pass"; the Spanish contrasts it with note 5's «ecuaciones de transferencia»); grid / cuadrícula; box / casilla; path / camino; conditional random field / campo aleatorio condicional; hidden Markov model / modelo oculto de Markov; transitions, emissions / transiciones, emisiones; treebank (English), glossed «corpus anotados a mano»; back-pointer / puntero hacia atrás; one at a time, left to right / una por una, de izquierda a derecha (never «una por vez»), and the chain it gives «la cadena con las etiquetas elegidas una por una»; greedy in English; passes over the training text are readings / lecturas, as in notes 6 and 7. «Por sí solo» for "alone" after a noun, since «solo» there reads as "only". A name's kind is «tipo» on the Spanish page (not «clase», which a scan for course words reads as a lesson); the softmax's «clase por secuencia» stays.

## Figures

- Colors: carmine is a name (its underline across its words, its kind small after them, as note 2 drew entities, for persons and places alike) and the labeling or chain asked about (its path through the grid, its boxes with a carmine border and a 1 px ring just outside it, so the ring takes nothing from the numbers); blue is scores and what flows (figure 2's boxes by their token's softmax share, the table A on one scale from its highest cell to its lowest, both mixed at most 50% into the card so that their ink text keeps its contrast in both themes (no switch to light text), figure 4's edges into the box picked as wide as their share, figure 6's back-pointers); ink the box picked (outlined; the keyboard's focus ring is blue, `.sl-box.picked:focus-visible`, so focus shows on the grid's one tab stop) and the table cells a labeling uses; faint the other edges, absent boxes (dashed) and O's code. A broken I- is a faint dashed underline, never carmine. Green, --pick, --coral and --heat-max are not used.
- One grid for figures 2 to 6 (`makeGrid` in the note's script): a column per token and a row per label, "P drawn on its side", each box an HTML button in a CSS grid, with an SVG layer of lines over its gaps measured from the boxes (`.fig svg.sl-edges`, which must override the figures' card background). A start dot before the first column, in a gap of its own (`.sl-grid .sl-rowhead` has 9, 6 and 6 px of right padding at full width, up to 480 px and up to 360 px, the dot halfway between the row names' text and the boxes, its radius 3.5, 2.5 and 2 px), named "the start, before the first word" in the keys of figures 3 to 6. Kind "pick": the arrow keys and clicks pick any box (figures 2, 4, 6), and an extra box at the right (Z, or the chain's end) sits under the last column on phones, where the lines into it leave their box to the right, run down the margin beside the column (kept inside the panel with the widest stroke) and come in from its right side, crossing no box, the box keeping its side padding, and down from the column's lowest box reaches it, up from it goes back; kind "path": each column is a group of radio buttons, up and down set its label, left and right move between columns (figures 3, 5). Codes of the current labeling small above the words, names underlined in the SVG layer. On screens up to 360 px the boxes' text is 0.6rem with −0.03em letter spacing and 3 px gaps, so five columns of "−28.43" fit at 320 px with the start dot's gap (`sweep8.mjs` measures each box's text against the inside of its border and ring, and wants half a pixel clear).
- Figure 1 (HTML word line): "Don Quixote of La Mancha" (I.I's title) and "Don Gaiferos Don Gaiferos , Marsilio Marsilio" (II.XXVI, the book's two names that touch), «don Quijote de la Mancha», «don Gaiferos don Gaiferos , Marsilio Marsilio»; each word a button whose click or Enter cycles O → B-PER → I-PER → B-LOC → I-LOC; Reset keeps its place while hidden (`.sl-ctl`). The readout gives the codes, the names read back ("a person, “Don Quixote”, and a place, “La Mancha”", the comma closing the first; "4 persons: …") and how many of 5^N labelings follow the scheme (5 tokens: 571 of 3,125; 7: 7,953 of 78,125, counted live), or the broken I- ("after B-LOC", or "at the start" / «al comienzo»), or that two names merged, said only when an I- stands where the phrase opened a name with B- (removing a name is no merge).
- Figure 2: the head trained alone, phrases "village of La Mancha" and "Don Quixote of La Mancha" («lugar de la Mancha», «don Quijote de la Mancha»), both grids built once; P to two decimals; the readout's softmax from the printed scores, its denominator wrapping between terms under the bar on phones (`.sl-ro .frac`). The columns of "of La Mancha" are identical in both phrases.
- Figure 3: the tagger's head (trained with A) on the same phrases, the starting labeling figure 2's per-token picks; the table A with its start row below, shaded and with the cells used outlined; the readout s(x, y) = (tokens' part) + (steps' part) = a + b = s, exact in two decimals, each term a piece that keeps together, and the weakest step and the best of the 5^N labelings (brute force). Its height is kept over the labelings one token from the start and from the best, the five with one label throughout, and the longest readouts of all 5^N (capitals counted 1.5); `fig3all.mjs` clicks through all 3,750 labelings at six widths in both languages and finds none taller.
- Figure 4: α on "village of La Mancha" from the two-decimal P and A, boxes as whole numbers from 100, two decimals below, "< 0.01" for less; readouts with terms to five significant digits and results as the boxes show them (four significant digits had missed the last digit); the Z box with the best labeling's p. A share of at least 99.95% reads "More than 99.9%", never "100.0%".
- Figures 5 and 6: the HMM on "To come to the point" (II.XLV, opens its sentence) and «la tomó por la mano» (I.XLII, decoded inside its sentence with the same tags), boxes only where the treebank gave the word that tag in at least 1 of 50 uses (rows PART ADP VERB DET NOUN; PRON DET VERB ADP NOUN PROPN), and decoding over them gives the same chain as over all 17 tags. Every log is a two-decimal value of the module, so every printed sum is exact. Figure 5's boxes show log P(x_i | h_i), a row under the grid labeled "step" / «paso» each step's log P(h_i | h_{i−1}) (the heading names both), and the readout the counts behind the step picked; the Spanish says «las 8 cadenas de la cuadrícula», not «posibles», since the other tags are possible but rare. Figure 6: modes Viterbi (log δ, back-pointers, the chain read back "along the back-pointers", the figure draws no arrows) and One at a time (greedy's own candidates given its own previous choice, never drawn over δ; its heading the sum it compares, its end box "total", never "best", and the panel's aria-label set by the mode).
- The opening's pictures are laid out by `pictures8.mjs` in the browser from the site's fonts (a space measured as the difference of "x x" and "xx", since a lone space measures 0): La Mancha twice with the first 12 of each word's 100 numbers as bars, identical, in strips 30 units wide on a line at 0, named once in the first row ("first 12 of / its 100 numbers", «12 primeros de / sus 100 números», 10.5 units, and "0" at the line's end), a place and a person; the clause with the decided words' tags and hooks back; a 4 × 5 grid with one path, 625 ways and 20 boxes.
- Its classes in `notes.css` start with `sl-`. Its data module, both books, is 6 KB.
- Checks, in `note8/w8/`: `sweep8.mjs` walks every state of the six figures at 1280, 390 and 320 px in both languages (822 grid states): each box's text fits, the grid fits its panel, column heads do not overlap, heights stay steady, the page never scrolls sideways, the arrow keys reach every box (a search from each box), and each readout's calculation, parsed from its text, gives its printed result: no problem left after the review's fixes.
