# Note 6, what word vectors capture: its figures and numbers

Planned on 10 Oct 2026 from class 2, slides 20 and 21, and class 3, slides 4 to 8 (three drafted plans merged by a judge, in `course-materials/natural-language-processing/note6/judge.md`), and built without a mockup round: Joaquín asked for the note to go ahead without his feedback. Its decisions follow the judge's recommendations, except where noted below. A review by workflow (five angles, each finding checked by a verifier that tried to refute it; 57 findings, 46 confirmed) corrected it before publishing; its results are in `note6/w6/review6-results.txt`.

## Notation

- θ is the angle between two vectors (the logistic function has been σ since note 5, as the page says); the cosine is written with v_w and v_{w′}, not the slide's u and v, which are note 5's context and target vectors; cos replaces the slide's undefined sim.
- The odd one out: w* = argmax_{w∈L} ‖∑_{w′∈L∖{w}} v_{w′}‖, L the list, on vectors scaled to length 1 ("scaled to length 1" is said in words; no hats); the three-vector identity is written for words w, w′ and w″ (‖v_w + v_{w′} + v_{w″}‖ = √(3 + 2(cos θ_{ww′} + cos θ_{ww″} + cos θ_{w′w″}))), since c is the context word and a, b the analogy's words on this page. Analogies: b* = argmax_{w∉{a, a*, b}} cos(v_w, v_b − v_a + v_{a*}), never w ∈ V, since V is a number; t = v_b − v_a + v_{a*}.
- V is the model's vocabulary (4,993; 5259), so both modes of figure 1 compare the same words; the book's 14,921 (22 605) different words are mentioned once. x is the text's vector in both places: the bag of words in ℝ^V (section 1) and the pooled vector in ℝ^d (section 6), which says so. e(w) = v_w is defined at pooling. N is a text's length.
- The classifier on top is written ŷ = softmax(U σ(Wx)), with no letter for the hidden layer, so h keeps the meaning the notes settled (logistic regression's output; README, "Notation"); the bias is folded into W by a 1 at the start of x, as note 4 did. The judge had recommended the slide's h = σ(Wx); keeping h free was decided here, from the README's settled notation, and is to be confirmed with Joaquín.
- The loss of section 2 is note 5's, referred to in words; only the observed pair's −log σ(u_cᵀv_o) is written, so ℒ is off the page. σ(u_adventuresᵀv_quest) and σ(u_adventuresᵀv_search) name their target.
- The text says "runs" for the five trainings ("in all five runs"; Spanish «entrenamientos»), and section 1 says that the figures and the numbers quoted show the first run; ranks that vary between runs are given as ranges (sister between 5th and 35th; hermana between 25th and 148th; sobrina beyond 3500th).
- Sentences that followed the slides' wording were rewritten in the note's own words (the review found five: "nobody told the model", "nobody programmed it", "pulls elsewhere and shortens the sum", "only geometry … cheese is food", "a choice of design").
- "Embedding" is the word for a word's vector from section 1 on, in English on both pages; «largo» is a vector's norm, as in note 3; «el cuarto excluido» is the Spanish name of the odd one out (the glossary's).

## Figures

- Colors: carmine is what is asked about or looked for (the picked word's arrow, strip and label in figure 1, both targets in figure 2, the odd one out's bar and word, an analogy's answer and its "?" tile, the picked word in figure 5, the first text's pooled strip); blue the vectors compared and the scores (the closest word's arrow and strip, every arc, cosine and sum-length bars, the second text's pooled strip); green (`--ok`) only figure 2's context words, note 5's convention; ink the drawn words and word labels; faint ink the dial's counts, the given words (struck through) and each word's numbers under a pooled strip. `--pick`, `--coral` and `--heat-max` are not used.
- Figure 1 (the dial, SVG): ten words on roving chips, modes Embedding and Bag of words. The picked word's vector along 0°, its closest word's at their true angle and relative length (capped at 1.25 times), a blue arc with the angle; around the rim, every other word of the vocabulary counted by degree as faint radial bars, named "words per degree", scaled so that the most words in one degree over all ten chips fill the ring; the rim's ends and top labeled 0°, 90°, 180° with their cosines; "angle from “curate”" under the rim's diameter. Under it, the two vectors' 100 numbers as bars (40 units per unit of value), each strip named "the 100 numbers of “curate”" with + and − at the left of its midline (Joaquín's rule that bars pointing two ways name each way). In Bag of words mode, one spike at 90° labeled "all 4,992 other words", both arrows of length 1, and each strip a single 1 at the word's place in the vocabulary, sorted by frequency ("“curate”: a 1 at place 157 of 4,993"). The readout's cosine is computed from the printed dot product and lengths, and θ from that cosine, so the readout, the arc's label and the fraction agree. The angle's label goes inside the angle (or just beyond the blue arrow when the angle is under 30°), then the closest word's label at the first of five places around its tip that covers no text and no arrow.
- Figure 2 (two targets and one context word, SVG): a designed example in two numbers per vector, presented as one: targets quest and search (asno and jumento), the shared context word adventures («rocinante»), one drawn word per target, combat and ought («lágrimas», «dejando», drawn from the book with β = ¾ by a seeded generator); the second mode replaces search's context word with finding («costal»), a word the book has near search and never near quest. Mean binary cross-entropy over the four pairs, η = 1.2 (note 5's rate), every vector moving; Back and Next, Reset at step 10; a new mode starts from step 0. The plane runs from −1.8 to 1.8 (every vector stays within ±1.69). Labels are placed by `stepLabels` (plane.js, moved there from note 5's figure 4 for this figure), with every label shown while their places are worked out, since a hidden label measures as empty. The readout's cosine is computed from the printed coordinates and lengths.
- Figure 3 (the odd one out, HTML): sixteen chips in four unlabeled rows of four (two to a row on phones), toggled into a list of three to six words; modes Scaled to length 1 and As trained. One row per listed word with its sum's length as a bar, named "length of the sum without it"; the longest in carmine, and every sum within 0.01 of it too, with a readout that says no word stands out. The sums come from the two-decimal cosines and lengths in the data module. The readout writes √(3 + 2(…)) for three vectors, √(2 + 2 · c) with a note naming the cosine for two, and √(n + 2 · S) with S, the sum of the cosines, in a note for more; the sum's terms may break before each plus sign; as trained, it gives only the length, and says when the winner is the list's shortest vector. Tied words are listed as “a”, “b” and “c” («a», «b» y «c», e before an i sound). Fewer than three words: "Pick at least three words." On phones a gap follows each group of four, so the groups show two to a row; the text names them (the mounts, the foods, the people, the island's government). The heights kept steady come from `oddsweep.mjs`, which measures every list of 3 to 6 words in both modes at every 20 px from 320 to 1280 in both languages; the readout's tallest lists are written into the script.
- Figure 4 (analogies, HTML): four analogies on roving chips, modes "Closest to b − a + a*" and "Closest to b". The six closest words that are not given, with any given word that ranks above the sixth kept at its rank, faint and struck through, marked "given"; cosines with three decimals (the given quixote scores 0.750 against sancho's 0.748). The answer is the first word not given, carmine, also in the "?" tile. The readout's split cosine prints its three terms and ‖t‖ with four decimals, so that the fraction gives the listed cosine in all eight states; for the analogy the book gets wrong it gives the answer's count in the book ("the book uses “sister” only 12 times"; hermana 11; brother is used 107 times, hermano 105). The label's t = v_b − v_a + v_{a*} never breaks.
- Figure 5 (the book and the web, HTML): eight words on roving chips, modes Trained on the book and Trained on the web; the five closest words with blue bars named "cosine with “curate”". The web's readout prints its dot product and lengths with three decimals, so that the fraction gives the listed cosine (ínsula's 0.34 with two decimals did not). The web lists drop misspellings that land near the word they misspell, screened by hand (`web6.mjs`'s TYPOS: helmut, in helmet's list).
- Figure 6 (pooling, HTML with SVG strips): four pairs of texts on roving chips, modes Mean and Maximum; each text's words as tiles and a strip of their numbers as faint dots, with the pooled vector over them as bars (40 units per unit of value); the cosine is computed live from the int8 vectors, its dot product and lengths printed with three decimals so that the fraction gives it, and "= 1, since x′ = x" is written, without numbers, when the two pooled vectors are the same. Pair 2's sentence pools its content words as the mode pools (means 0.25, maxima 0.25; Spanish 0.24 and 0.25).
- The opening's pictures are hand-made SVG: helmet and basin (yelmo and bacía) at their angle, 28° (25°), with a faint arrow at helmet's median angle to the rest of the vocabulary, 74° (71°), labeled "a typical word"; rocinante, dapple, mule and island (rocinante, rucio, jumento, ínsula), island filled carmine; knight → knights and squire → squires (caballero, escudero), the answer carmine.
- Its classes in `notes.css` start with `wv-`; on screens up to 400 px the rows of figures 3 to 5 share their columns (subgrid), the words' as wide as the list's longest word, so no word breaks. Its data module, both books, is 34 KB.
- `sweep6.mjs` (in `note6/w6/`) checks every state of figures 1 and 2 at 1280, 390 and 320 px in both languages: texts inside the drawing and clear of each other, labels off the arrows and off the points: 252 states, no problem. `readouts6.mjs` checks from the data module that every printed calculation gives the result it prints (72 readouts).

## Numbers

- The model: skip-gram with negative sampling as word2vec.c trains it (`book-vectors/sgns.mjs`), d = 100, radius drawn from 1 to m = 5, k = 5 with β = ¾, 20 readings (the program's default is 5), subsampling t = 10⁻⁴ (not on the page), minimum count 5, learning rate 0.025 falling linearly; five trainings, seeds 1 to 5; the figures show seed 1. gensim with the same settings gives the same neighbors (planning's check).
- Vocabulary: 4,993 words (95.7% of 404,923 tokens) and 5259 (92.7% of 376 597); the 9,928 words seen fewer than 5 times are 66.5% of the 14,921 different words and 4.3% of the tokens (17 346, 76.7% of 22 605, 7.3%; the page says "three in four"). All pairs of the vocabulary: median angle 66.4° (64.4°). Lengths 1.18 to 4.48 (1.19 to 3.73); median 1.60 for words seen 5 to 9 times and 2.61 for 50 to 199 (1.57 and 2.61). curate: 51 negative numbers, 49 positive, no zeros (cura 58 and 42); 16 of the other 4,992 words beyond 90° (10 of 5258).
- Figure 1, closest words (the first run, cosine, angle): curate → barber 0.822, 35° (cura → barbero 0.852, 32°; 5.92/(2.53 · 2.84) and 6.28/(2.50 · 2.95)); helmet → basin 0.883, 28° (yelmo → mambrino, bacía second in all five trainings); summer → winter 0.900, 26° (verano → invierno 0.949, 18°), first in all five trainings in both books; bread → cheese first in all five (pan → pedazo, then queso, cebolla); giants → monsters, windmills second (gigantes → malandrines, molinos fourth). Medians of the chips' angles 68° to 75°.
- Section 2: curate and barber within five words of each other 42 times (cura and barbero 45). quest 46 times, search 45, never within 20 words (closest 75 apart); in quest of adventures 10 times, in search of adventures 3; quest is search's closest word in all five trainings, cos 0.76 to 0.80; σ(u_adventuresᵀv) 0.88 and 0.75. asno 86, jumento 73, never within 10 words (closest 12), sobre su asno and sobre su jumento 5 times each; each the other's closest in at least four trainings (jumento for asno in 5, asno for jumento in 4), cos 0.80 to 0.85, 33.7° on seed 1; σ(u_rocinanteᵀv) 0.82 and 0.90. Goldberg and Levy (2014) for the collapse without negatives.
- Figure 2's example: start 110681 of `rank-toy.mjs` (the good starts of `toy6.mjs`: 130 to 150° at the start, closing at every step to 15 to 40°, the control wider than 100°), chosen for marks that stay apart; the readout's angle (from the two-decimal cosine of the printed coordinates) 140°, 126, 106, 83, 59, 44, 33, 27, 24, 22, 20° in the shared mode, and 140° to 138°, wavering by a degree, with a word of its own.
- Figure 3: default bread, cheese, onions, island (pan, queso, cebolla, ínsula): scaled, island's sum 2.75 against 2.54 without onions (ínsula 2.83, then 2.55 without queso); as trained, onions wins with 7.39 (cebolla 6.92), the shortest vector of the list, 1.66 (1.55), in all five trainings. Three words of a row and one of another: 960 of 960 right scaled over five trainings, 82.8% as trained (79.1%; the page says 83% and 79%). Of the 14,756 lists of 3 to 6 words, 1,975 scaled and 505 as trained have their two longest sums within 0.01 (1666 and 688). The names list sancho, quixote, dulcinea, teresa, rocinante (four people and a horse): the longest sum leads the third by 0.023 to 0.054 (0.014 to 0.041), and its word changes between runs (teresa, rocinante, dulcinea, rocinante, teresa; teresa, dulcinea, dulcinea, teresa, teresa). The rule on unit vectors is the paper's: the word farthest from the list's average (Mikolov, Chen, Corrado and Dean 2013, section 5).
- Figure 4 (rank of the answer over five trainings; rank among the words closest to b alone): knight : knights :: squire : squires 1,1,1,1,1; 408 (caballero, escuderos 1610), the steps' cosine 0.68 (0.69); sancho : dapple :: quixote : rocinante 1,1,5,2,1 (rucio, all first), dapple, a given word, scoring 0.752 above it; knight : squire :: quixote : sancho first in all, already quixote's closest in four runs (closest-to-b ranks 1, 1, 1, 1, 5), quixote itself 0.750 against 0.748 (caballero : escudero :: quijote : sancho first in all, sancho among quijote's three closest, ranks 1, 3, 2, 3, 2; 0.770 and 0.736); son : daughter :: brother : sister 5, 11, 9, 35, 13 (hijo : hija :: hermano : hermana 106, 25, 45, 35, 148). The planned failing case brother : sister :: son was dropped: the words closest to son include a slur. Searching every word, a given word comes first in 22 of 25 analogies (24 of 25), over the planning's probes. The paper discards the question words; Mikolov, Yih and Zweig (2013) found the regularities in a recurrent network's vectors.
- Section 5 and figure 5: fastText cc.en.300 and cc.es.300 (Grave et al. 2018; Common Crawl and Wikipedia, CBOW with character n-grams, 2,000,000 words, d = 300, CC BY-SA 3.0); closest words searched among the table's 100,000 most frequent lowercase words; counted over the whole table (`coverage6.mjs`; the first count, on the 200,000-word slice, was wrong), 4,839 of the book's 4,993 words in lowercase, the 154 missing mostly names and words with an apostrophe (58 of them) (5157 of 5259, the 102 missing names and old forms such as mochachos and juridición).
