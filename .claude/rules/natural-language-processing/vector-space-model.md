# Note 3, the vector space model: its figures and numbers

## Figures

- A document is a chapter of the book, labeled as `notes-natural-language-processing.md` says.
- A query, what the reader looks for, is carmine: its arrow and handle on the plane, the pressed words of the search, the opening's query. Vectors and scores are blue: a chapter's arrow, the arc of the angle, the bars of a ranking. Chapters are faint ink points until picked or ranked, then ink. A chapter's word weights are carmine bars, as note 2's counts.
- The pipeline's optional steps are toggle buttons (`data-step`, any of them pressed at once), not modes; a dropped token stays faint and struck through, a token its lemma changed is carmine. The readout gives the whole book's tokens and different words after the steps pressed, and its sentence what the last step changed.
- The plane of two words (`wordPlane` in `vector-space-model.js`): `createPlane` from 0 to the largest count rounded up to ten, its axis letters replaced by the two words; every chapter a dot. Figure 2 picks a chapter by a click within 30 units of its dot, or with the arrow keys on the drawing (Right and Up the next chapter in the book's order, Left and Down the previous one, wrapping around), starting at I.VIII. Figure 4's query is a handle starting at (6, 2). It ranks points, not chapters: chapters with the same counts share a point and its rank (on knight and squire the 126 chapters sit on 52 points, 9 of them at (1, 0)), and ties between points go to the one whose first chapter comes first in the book. By angle the three closest points get arrows from the origin and the closest an arc, and a point with neither word, having no direction, is left out; by distance, dashed lines from the query's tip. Each of the three tied with the closest gets a label, its first chapter and how many more share the point ("I.XXXVIII +8"), and the readout names the others tied ("tied with II.LIII", "tied with 8 more"), since Joaquín found a tie at equal distance shown with one label.
- Chapter labels in figures 2 and 4 are placed by `placeLabel` in `vector-space-model.js`, because `placeAlong` only flips a label to the other side of its point, which on an axis is the axis too (Joaquín found the chosen chapter's label missing on the x axis, where 32 chapters of knight and squire lie). It tries eight places around the point (centered above, below or beside it, or in a corner), just clear of the dot and then twice farther, starting from a preferred direction (figure 2 along the chapter's vector; figure 4 beside the point by angle, away from the query by distance), and takes the first where the whole label lies inside the plane, off the axis lines and clear of the axis names (by 12 units, so a label never reads as part of one), the query's handle and the labels already placed; in a crowded corner it takes the place inside the plane that overlaps least. `label-audit.mjs`, in the scratchpad of session `a9b07105-3845-4107-8a8b-b935b89b4dcf`, checks every chapter of figure 2 and the query over both views of figure 4, in both rankings, at 1280 and 390 px.
- The query is read as a tiny document, never as a word (Joaquín had asked whether the figure compared a word with chapters): the paragraph before the figure says its point is placed by how many times it uses each of the two words ((6, 2), as if it said sancho six times and quixote twice), and the readout reads both points as counts, "Its counts, (9, 3), are in the same proportion as the query's, (6, 2), whatever their size" by angle ("nearly the same proportion" unless exactly proportional) and "are close to the query's, whatever their proportion" by distance.
- Figure 4 starts in a close-up of the plane's corner at the origin, a third of each axis (0 to 20 for sancho and quixote, 0 to 10 for knight and squire, ticks every unit), where most chapters lie (111 of the 126 within 10 of knight and squire) and the query's nearest chapters are a few units apart: on the whole plane the differences between angle and distance were too small to see (Joaquín's request). Whole plane, its other view button, shows figure 2's plane. The close-up is clipped at its frame: chapters beyond it are cut off, the handle stays inside it, and a closest chapter beyond it gets no label (its arrow or line runs off the edge), while the readout's sentence says so ("and runs off the close-up").
- The weights figure: five chapters as mode buttons named by their adventure, four weightings as mode buttons, and the chapter's ten heaviest words as a roving list with bars scaled to the first (ties in alphabetical order). Counts show no decimals, the other weightings two.
- The search figure: sixteen query words per language as toggle chips, the first (windmills, molinos) pressed at the start; the first five chapters with their titles and blue bars scaled to the first; BM25 at the start, with k₁ (0.5, 1.2, 3) and b (0, 0.75, 1) as rows of mode buttons hidden, keeping their space, under the cosine. The cosine weighs a chapter's words by log tf times idf and the query's words by idf (each counted once). BM25 scores show two decimals, cosines three. BM25 uses the slide's idf log(N/nᵢ), with one sentence on the smoothed form (Joaquín's decision, as are the query words as buttons and the cosine before BM25).
- Its classes in `notes.css` start with `vs-`; its data module, both books, is 39 KB.

## Numbers

- **The same books, cut into their 126 chapters** (52 in part I, 74 in part II), each chapter a document, tokenized as in note 1. Part II's front matter (title page, dedication, prologue) belongs to no chapter, so the chapters hold 402,944 English tokens and 372,885 Spanish ones, 1,979 and 3,712 fewer than note 1's counts.
- Stopwords: Snowball's lists, 174 English words (161 of them in the book) and 308 Spanish ones (262): 1.08% and 1.17% of the book's different words, 54.9% and 52.0% of its tokens; without them the text keeps 45.1% and 48.0% (181,567 and 178,903 tokens). - The first line of chapter I, to the end of its second clause, so that lemmatizing changes words on both pages (no word of the first clause alone changes in English, which Joaquín noticed): "In a village of La Mancha, the name of which I have no desire to call to mind, there lived not long since one of those gentlemen", 27 tokens, where the list turns lived into live, gentlemen into gentleman and those into that; "En un lugar de la Mancha, de cuyo nombre no quiero acordarme, no ha mucho tiempo que vivía un hidalgo", 20 tokens, where it turns un into uno, la into el, quiero into querer, ha into haber, vivía into vivir, and Mancha and nombre into the verbs manchar and nombrar.
