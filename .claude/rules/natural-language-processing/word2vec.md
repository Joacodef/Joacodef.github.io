# Note 5, word2vec: its figures and numbers

## Figures

- Colors: the target word carmine (its tile, its row of W_in, its vector's arrow), the context words it was seen with green (`--ok`, the third class), the drawn words ink; lines and curves blue (the line u·v_o = 0, the curve of P(w)). An n-gram the other order lacks is outlined in carmine, as note 2 outlines a word in only one of two groups; its key says "not in the other order", naming no speaker, since carmine is Sancho's color in note 4.
- Notation decided with Joaquín: the hidden layer is written v_o (v_o = W_inᵀx), so h keeps note 4's meaning, h = P(+ | o, c) = σ(u_cᵀv_o); the softmax's sum runs over w (∑_{w=1}^V), so k is only the number of negatives; the negatives' normalizer sums over w′; N is the text's length (slide 13 writes n); f is only a frequency and d only the dimension, so slide 13's "f(x(d)) = f(x(πd))" is said in words; σ is note 4's θ; in negative sampling S also holds the drawn pairs, y = 0. Each word w has two vectors, v_w as a target and u_w as a context word; o, c, S and |S| are defined in section 2, before figure 2 uses them.
- Figure 1 (order and n-grams): two rows of mode buttons, the order (Sancho's, Don Quixote's) and n (1, 2, 3); the line as tiles; n = 1 lists the counts in alphabetical order, so both orders look the same; n = 2 and 3 list the n-grams in order, outlined in carmine where the other order lacks them.
- Figure 2 (skip-grams): the line's tiles are a roving list; the target is filled carmine, its context green, and the word picked has a 2 px ink border (the outline is left to keyboard focus); m on mode buttons (1, 2, 3); the pairs as chips with their j; the readout sums every word's pairs, the target's term in carmine, with singular forms and "nothing comes after it" at the line's ends.
- Figure 3 (the forward pass): two tables, W_in with x (its rows the roving buttons that pick the target) and each column u of W_out written across its word's row with z and p(c | o), the bar column named "probability p(c | o)"; scores computed from the printed entries in whole hundredths (so 3.455 rounds to 3.46); bars scaled so that 0.5 fills the track (the largest p is 0.49); the readout follows the target's likeliest neighbor in the line, two lines (the dot product, then the softmax), with ≈ where values are rounded.
- Figure 4 (negative sampling): the plane of two-number vectors, −1.8 to 1.8 (ticks −1, 0, 1; every vector stays within −1.63 and 1.42); the shaded side of the blue line is where a context vector makes its pair's h larger than 0.5; Back and Next take gradient steps; word labels quoted, in the sans. Label places are chosen for all steps at once by a path search (a turn of more than a quarter around the point between steps costs much), and the labels are placed in turn and then again with all the others fixed; a place costs much for covering a label, a point or the arrow, for lying across a zero line, for being nearer another mark (point or arrow tip) than its own, and for stretching over another point. `mock/sweep.mjs` (1,632 checks) reports only accepted states: “habit” across the vertical zero line at step 5 at 1280 px and at steps 8–10 on phones; «atrevió» across it at step 8 at 1280 px and at steps 4 and 8–10 on phones, and across the horizontal one at step 1 at 320 px; “windmills” across the horizontal one at step 10 on English phones; and figure 4 growing at step 10 on phones, when Reset appears and wraps the controls (the site's convention for Back/Next figures). The key is laid out again once the fonts arrive. While steadyHeight measures the readout over every step, the buttons and focus are left alone, so a change of width keeps keyboard focus where it was.
- Figure 5 (the negatives' distribution): log–log axes as in note 1, with its minor ticks (`logChart` in plane.js, moved there from note 1 for this figure); the chart is drawn again only when β changes; β on mode buttons (0, ¾, 1), β = 1 faint behind the others and named "β = 1, by frequency" under its tail; "the" and "windmills" marked (que and molinos), their labels quoted, at the cheapest of 48 spots right of the y axis and off the curves; ten draws per set, ten sets per β, made offline with a seeded generator; Draw again cycles them.
- The opening's second picture shows the target's four neighbors and the likeliest word outside its window, faint; its aria-label says "almost nothing" ("casi nada") for that word only below 0.02, otherwise "much less" (Spanish «viento» gets 0.056).
- Its classes in `notes.css` start with `w2v-`; readouts whose height is kept steady are `display: flow-root` (`.w2v-ro`), so their last margin stays inside. The opening's picture texts get their sizes inline from `pictures.mjs`, which lays the pictures out from them. Its data module, both books, is 15 KB.

## Numbers

- **Sancho's line** at the windmills, chapter VIII of part I: "what we see there are not giants but windmills" (9 words, token 16,309 of the translation) and «aquellos que allí se parecen no son gigantes, sino molinos de viento» (12 words, token 15,068 of the original). Don Quixote's reading swaps giants and windmills (gigantes and molinos de viento). Bigrams shared by the two orders: 5 of 8 (8 of 11); trigrams 4 of 7 (6 of 10); orders of the words 9! = 362,880 (12! = 479,001,600). Section 2 explains the name with see (allí), whose pair (see, what) skips we ((allí, aquellos) skips que).
- The book's n-grams over the whole token stream, different (seen once): English words 14,921 (36.7%), bigrams 143,448 (71.5%, 9.6 times the words, "almost ten"), trigrams 305,757 (88.0%), 4-grams 377,659 (96.0%); Spanish 22,605 (48.7%), 152,433 (75.1%, 6.7 times, «casi siete»), 295,186 (89.8%), 354,497 (96.6%). Skip-gram pairs over the stream (2mN − m(m + 1)): m = 1, 2, 3 give 809,844, 1,619,686 and 2,429,526 (753,192, 1,506,382, 2,259,570); the opening says Don Quixote alone yields over a million and a half examples (m = 2). The line's pairs: m = 1, 2, 3 give 16, 30, 42 (22, 42, 60).
- Figure 3's model: the full softmax, d = 3, trained by gradient descent (8,000 steps, η = 0.5, L2 0.003, not mentioned on the page) on the line's pairs with m = 2, the best of 40 seeds (32; Spanish 10), its numbers rounded to two decimals as the tables show them: loss 1.289 against the lowest possible 1.236 (Spanish 1.386 and 1.279), the lowest being the guess that gives each word its share of the target's pairs; interior words give 0.21 to 0.27 to each of their four neighbors; "giants" gives its neighbors 0.97 (are 0.24, not 0.23, but 0.25, windmills 0.25), «gigantes» 0.90 (viento, outside its window, 0.056). With d = 2 the Spanish line fitted badly («viento» got more than «molinos»), so d = 3.
- Figure 4: the pair (giants, windmills) and three words drawn from the book with β = ¾, skipping the line's words: represented, habit, favours (detenido, atrevió, casarse); starting vectors from seed 16978, coordinates within 0.9; 10 steps. The data module stores η = 0.3 on the sum of the four pairs' losses, which is η = 1.2 on ℒ, their mean, as the page says ("with a learning rate η = 1.2"); the text gives each pair's term of the gradient and says ℒ's gradient averages them, and the summary divides by |S| (g = (h - y) / len(cs)). h of the observed pair 0.43 → 0.93; the readout's ℒ = −¼[…], worked from the printed h, goes 0.69, 0.62, 0.55, 0.48, 0.40, 0.32, 0.25, 0.18, 0.13, 0.10, 0.07.
- Figure 5: β = 1 gives the ten most frequent words 25.1% of the draws (Spanish 28.7%; the text names the first four, the, and, to, of and que, y, de, la) and the words seen once 1.35% (2.92%); β = ¾ 9.8% and 5.4% (9.9% and 10.6%); β = 0 0.07% and 36.7% (0.04% and 48.7%), which section 5 quotes as "more than a third" («casi la mitad»). P(“the”) 5.12% → 1.72% (que 5.42% → 1.64%); windmills 0.00247% → 0.00559% (×2.26; molinos ×1.94). The curves cross at f = (N/∑f^¾)⁴ = 261.8 (170.2): dulcinea, 261 uses, rank 172, is the first word that β = ¾ draws more often than β = 1 (modo, 168, rank 231).
- From the papers, checked against them: word2vec has two models, CBOW and the skip-gram (Mikolov, Chen, Corrado and Dean 2013); the original program draws each target's radius from 1 to m and saves only W_in (syn0); negative sampling and the ¾ come from the second word2vec paper, late in 2013 (Mikolov, Sutskever, Chen, Corrado and Dean), with k from 5 to 20 on small corpora and as small as 2 to 5 on large ones, tested on a billion words of news with a vocabulary of 692,000 words; some systems add W_in and W_out (GloVe). Training is stochastic gradient descent, one step per pair per reading of the text.
- Computed by `w2v/w2v-data.mjs` (the data module; `--facts` prints the numbers above), `w2v/shares.mjs`, `w2v/toy3.mjs` and `w2v/ns-sim.mjs`, from the books in `books/`, all in `course-materials/natural-language-processing/handoff-note5/` (private). The mockup is in its `mock/` (`build-w2v.mjs` and `pictures.mjs`, which draws the opening's pictures and the n-gram table for both the mockup and the site; `serve.mjs` on port 8096), its checks are `mock/sweep.mjs` (every state of every figure; W2V_EN and W2V_ES set to the site's URLs check the site), `mock/shots.mjs` and `mock/shots-states.mjs`, and `tools/stage5.mjs` moved the mockup into the site.
