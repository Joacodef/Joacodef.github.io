# Note 2, words, lemmas and WordNet: its figures and numbers

- WordNet is drawn down the left: a chain of hypernyms joined by blue lines, and kinds or parts branching from one blue spine, so the arrow keys follow the reading order. Its classes in `notes.css` start with `wd-`; its data module is 14 KB.
- Its two bar charts name what their bars measure, once, in a small line right-aligned over the bars (`.wd-measure`, added on 10 Oct 2026 for the rule that every chart names its axes): the vocabularies, "vocabulary size" (tamaño del vocabulario), over the page's own book; the senses, "uses in the book" (usos en el libro), above the first origin.
- Its lexical resources, named with their licenses in a line after its summary: Snowball stemmers in JavaScript (`snowball-stemmers` 0.6.0, ISC), lemma lists for Spanish and English (github.com/michmech/lemmatization-lists, ODbL), and Princeton WordNet 3.0 with the Spanish wordnet of the Multilingual Central Repository 3.0 (CC BY 3.0), both from NLTK's data packages `wordnet` and `omw-1.4`. None is installed in the site; copies are in the d233705d scratchpad (`nlpdeps/`, `dl/lemmas/`, `dl/wordnet/`).
- **The same books**, the Spanish page on the original and the English page on Ormsby's translation, counted as in note 1.
- A word's forms are the book's words that Michal Měchura's lemma lists (github.com/michmech/lemmatization-lists, ODbL) put under its lemma, split into lexeme and ending where the forms' shared beginning ends (three letters or more), else where Snowball's stem ends; *decir*'s forms split at its seven lexeme shapes, dec-, dic-, dig-, dij-, dir-, dich- and di. Spanish: *decir* 42 forms (dijo 1,804, decir 574, dicho 360, digo 322), *hablar* 30, *caballero* 3 (caballera 1), *hermoso* 4, *con* 1 (rank 11). English: *say* 4 (said 2,609, say 881, saying 254, says 139), *speak* 5, *knight* 3 (knighting 1, from the verb), *fair* 3, *with* 1 (rank 15). *Diremos* (6) is "we will say".
- Stem and lemma groups, Snowball stems (snowball-stemmers 0.6.0) against the lists' lemmas. Spanish: *dijo* (stem dij: dijo, dije, dijes 'trinkets', dijiste; lemma decir: 42), *hablaba* (stem habl: 38, the lemma's 30 and 8 forms with an attached pronoun, such as hablarle and hablándole), *caballeros* (3 and 3), *universidad* (stem univers: 3, with universo; lemma: 2), *fue* (stem: 1; lemmas ir 45 and ser 44; chapter I has both, "Fue luego a ver su rocín" and "lo primero que hizo fue limpiar unas armas"). English: *said* (stem: 1; lemma say: 4), *speaking* (stem: 3; lemma: 5, with spoke and spoken), *knights* (stem: 5, with knightly and knight's; lemma: 3), *university* (stem: 5, with universe, universal and universally; lemma: 2), *saw* (stem: 2, with saws, 'proverbs', as in "the silly sayings of the rich pass for saws in the world"; the list's lemma, see: 5).
- Vocabularies: Spanish 22,605 words, 10,032 stems, 12,596 lemmas (each word under the first lemma the list gives, or itself); English 14,921, 9,065 and 10,337. The lists hold only forms that differ from their lemma, so 9,861 Spanish and 9,302 English words are missing from them, mostly lemmas already; the Spanish list also lacks forms with an attached pronoun (decirle 29) and the book's old spellings (agora 147, mesmo 216, priesa 73, fermosa 16), so the true count of Spanish lemmas is lower.
- Tags, by hand, in Universal Dependencies' parts of speech: the first line of chapter I and the chapter's title in each language, with the entities la Mancha (a place) and Don Quixote of La Mancha / don Quijote de la Mancha (a person). *Del* is one token, tagged ADP. The constituent tree is the note's own sentence, "The squire rides a donkey" / "El escudero monta un asno".
- WordNet 3.0 (Princeton): 117,659 synsets and 147,306 different words and phrases (155,287 counting a word once per part of speech; the slide gives 147,306). The chain above knight.n.01 (10238375) has 11 synsets up to entity (knight, male aristocrat, aristocrat, leader, person, organism, living thing, whole, object, physical entity, entity); knight has 6 kinds, of which the figure shows knight-errant, Knight of the Round Table and Knight Templar. body_armor.n.01 (02862048, suit of armor) has 25 parts; the figure shows ten from head to foot (helmet, gorget, epauliere, breastplate, vambrace, cubitiere, gauntlet, cuisse, knee piece, solleret). The Spanish words come from the Multilingual Central Repository 3.0 (CC BY 3.0, via the Open Multilingual Wordnet), cuisse being muslera and quijote; the five synsets it leaves without a word take the note's own names (aristócrata, aristócrata varón, caballero andante, caballero de la Mesa Redonda, templario), and every definition, in both languages, is the note's own. *Light* has 47 senses: 15 as a noun, 6 as a verb, 25 as an adjective and 1 as an adverb.
- Senses, labeled by hand use by use: *light*, 98 uses, from the root *leuk- (brightness 68, a lamp or a flame 3, to kindle 2) and from *legwh- (not heavy 15, to come upon 6, to make light of 4), after etymonline's entries for light; *real*, 47 uses, from Latin realis, from res (verdadero 15), and regalis, from rex (del rey 21, una moneda 11), the DLE's two entries real¹ and real².
- Computed by `nlp/words-data.mjs` (forms, groups and vocabularies, into `nlp/words.json`), `nlp/note2-data.mjs` (the data module, which checks every synset and relation against WordNet and every Spanish word against the MCR) and `nlp/facts-note2.mjs` (every number the pages quote), with `nlp/wn.mjs`, `nlp/note2-hand.json` (tags, names, definitions, quoted lines) and `senses/labels.json` (each use's sense), in the d233705d scratchpad, from its `dl/lemmas/`, `dl/wordnet/` and `nlpdeps/`.
