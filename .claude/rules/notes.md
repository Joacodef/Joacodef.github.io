# Study notes

These rules hold for the notes of every course. Each course also has a rules file of its own in this folder, `notes-<course>.md` (its sources, notation, terms and figure conventions), and each note a file in `<course>/` (its examples, the numbers its page quotes and its figures' specifics); `plane-js.md` describes the shared helpers. Each loads when you open a file it covers.

## Who the notes are for

- A reader who never took the course, and Joaquín studying for it. Every page must make sense to someone who has only the page: never assume the reader knows the course's names, data or numbering, such as the frame numbers of a data set ("image 40") or the variable names of a lab.
- NEVER, anywhere in a note (text, readouts, captions, summary, `aria-label`s), refer to material outside the notes: the course's labs, notebooks, slides, classes, activities, readings, exams or professor ("as in class", "the lab's data set"), not even in a passing phrase that introduces a tool: "NLTK, the library of the course's first lab" is wrong, "NLTK, a Python library for working with text" is right (Joaquín's rule). The course appears only in the footer, through the `source` field. Links to other notes of the site are fine. Before showing a mockup or asking to commit, scan the pages and the figure scripts' strings for course words (course/curso, lab, slide, class/clase, notebook, professor); `tools/outside-refs.mjs` in `course-materials/natural-language-processing/handoff-note5/` does this.
- The course materials set what a note covers, keep its theory faithful to the class, including conventions that change results (such as the direction and order of rotations), and give its formula notation, which stays as in class (λm = PM, m for points, ℓ for lines) so the notes also serve for studying.
- Everything else is the note's own, chosen to make the concepts clear, which matters more than reproducing the class: the names of things, the numbers, the examples and the data behind the figures. The course's examples can suggest what to show, but they are not reproduced, unless the course's rules file makes an exception. When another course convention would confuse a new reader, prefer the clearer choice and tell Joaquín.
- Before writing or editing a note, read the course's README, `course-materials/<course>/README.md`, and the materials and reference files it lists for the note's subject: they set what the note covers, its theory and its formula notation. The labs show what the class computes, not what the page must reproduce.
- Never copy slides, lab or notebook code, assignment or activity text, or a book's text into `src/`. Summarize concepts in your own words and credit the source.

## Adding a note

- Put it in a course folder, e.g. `src/notes/computer-vision/2d-transformations.html`.
- Front matter: `title`, `module`, `order`, `description` (one sentence, shown on the notes index and on the home page's cards) and `scripts` if it has interactive figures.
- The notes index lists each course's notes in order, each run of consecutive notes that share a `module` under the module's name, so give the notes of one part of a course the same `module`, in both languages.
- `order` sets a note's place in its course's sequence: the notes index numbers the notes by it, and each note shows "Note N of M" in its breadcrumbs and links to the previous and next notes of its course (`noteSequence`). Give the Spanish version the same `order`.
- The homepage shows three cards: the first note of each course, then the second of each, and so on (`firstNotes`).
- Page-specific scripts go in `src/assets/js/notes/<course>/<note-slug>.js` and import helpers from `../../plane.js`. Add reusable helpers to `plane.js` instead of copying code between notes.
- Record the note's examples and every number its page quotes in its own rules file, `.claude/rules/<course>/<note-slug>.md`, scoped with `paths` to its two pages and its scripts.
- To add a course, follow "Adding a course" in `course-materials/README.md`.

## Spanish version

- Every note ships in English and Spanish; plan both from the start. The Spanish page is `src/es/notes/<course>/<same-slug>.html` (URL `/es/notes/<course>/<slug>/`), with the same markup, figure ids and `scripts`, and translated `title`, `module` and `description`. `src/es/es.json` sets `lang: es` for everything below `src/es/`.
- The layouts pair the two versions by URL (`otherLanguage` in `eleventy.config.js`), show the "Leer en español" or "Read in English" link at the top of the note, and add `hreflang` links. The note layout's own text in both languages is in `src/_data/i18n.json`. The site header and footer stay in English, marked `lang="en"` on Spanish pages.
- Figure scripts are shared by both pages. Every sentence and label a reader sees or hears, including `aria-label`s and SVG text, is written with `tr(en, es)` from `plane.js`, so the two versions sit side by side. A Spanish readout never shows English.
- Numbers keep the decimal point in Spanish too (3.1823, not 3,1823), as in the English notes and in NumPy (Joaquín's choice, although the Apuntes write decimal commas). English sets thousands apart with a comma (1,024; 10,000); Spanish with a non-breaking space from five digits on (10&nbsp;000), and not at all in four (2000). A percentage takes no space in either language (46%). `localNum` in `plane.js` writes numbers this way.
- Use the course's own Spanish terms, from its materials and the glossary in its course README. Address the reader with tú ("Arrastra", "Usa las flechas del teclado"). A note is an apunte (el apunte de TD), not a nota.
- An edit to one version is mirrored in the other in the same change, and the width, theme and keyboard checks run on both.

## Structure of a note

- Open without formulas. Before section 1, lead a reader who has never seen the subject into it: say in plain words what question the note answers or what it makes possible, and why it matters, with a small picture when one explains it. The homogeneous coordinates note does this with `.asks`, small pictures of its questions, each with a caption. The first formula appears inside a section. Everything in the opening fits a 320px-wide screen.
- Then numbered `<section>`s whose `<h2>` starts with `<span class="n">N</span>`.
- Every numbered section except the summary has an interactive example of its own: a `.fig` right after the text that introduces the section's idea, in which the reader changes something (drags a point, edits a vector, turns a view) and sees that idea respond, with the computation in the readout. Plan each section around what the reader does in its figure; an idea that gives the reader nothing to do belongs in the section it follows from.
- End with a `.sum` summary that pairs each formula with its NumPy equivalent. Notes have no "A worked example" section: concrete numbers go in the sections and figures where they explain an idea.
- Older notes that still open with `.duo` formulas, or have sections without a figure, are not the model to copy: reworking one brings it to these rules, its figures planned with mockups first.
- Name what an example uses by what it is, such as a view by where its camera stands, never by its number in a course's data set. Buttons that restore a figure to its first state are labeled "Reset" ("Reiniciar" in Spanish).
- Walk the reader's path before writing any text, caption or figure slide: list what the course's earlier notes introduced (definitions, formulas, algorithms, worlds, examples) and what the page has already said above that point, captions, figure lead-ins and slides included. Introduce each thing once, where the reader first meets it; afterwards refer back ("the cliff of the Q-learning note") and spend the words on what is new. A figure's first slide does not restate the paragraph above it. When an introduction moves (a world first shown in a later note, a figure added to an earlier one), move it and turn the later ones into references. Terms and examples stay consistent across notes.
- The running text is justified on screens wider than 560 px, with long words hyphenated in the page's language (Joaquín's choice): the paragraphs and lists directly inside a `.prose` block, so put body text there. The header, figure readouts, captions and cards keep their alignment. Phones keep the text left-aligned, Joaquín's choice after comparing both: at about 40 characters a line, justifying widened one space in ten by more than 6 px and spread the words before a formula too long to share their line (a `.nowrap` span) across the whole width.

## Math markup

- Components in `notes.css`: `.disp` for display math, `.col` for column vectors, `.mat` for matrices (3 columns unless `style="--cols: N"`; `mat()` in `plane.js` writes one), `.frac` for fractions, `sup.t` for the transpose and other superscripts, `.pt` for points (carmine) and `.ln` for lines (blue). A note's key formulas are `.disp.hero-f`, set larger; at desktop width they stay on one line, and one too long for that at the hero size, such as Sarsa's update, adds `long`, which sets it smaller.
- Write an estimate's hat with `.hat` around the letter (`<span class="hat"><i>M</i></span>`, and `class="hat lo"` for a lowercase letter), not with a combining circumflex, which the serif font sets beside the letter. In SVG labels, draw the hat as a text of its own over the letter, as the multiple views figure does. Combining tildes (`&#771;`) render correctly.
- A heading that contains math wraps its text in a `<span>` after the number (`<h2><span class="n">4</span><span>Estimating <i>H</i> …</span></h2>`): the `h2` is a flex row, and loose text and `<i>` would become separate items with gaps between them.
- Variables in `<i>`, subscripts in `<sub>`, and the real minus sign `−` (U+2212) in numbers.
- Use the course's formula notation, as its rules file describes. In code snippets, name variables after the formulas' symbols (`m1` for m₁, `mat_a` for A), so code and math read alike.
- Wrap each side of a long equation in a `.nowrap` span, putting the `=` at the start of the right-hand side (`<span class="nowrap">= …</span>`). On narrow screens the line then breaks before an equals sign, never after it. Do the same in figure readouts.

## Interactive figures

- Every chart names its axes, in the figures and in the opening's small pictures alike: what an axis measures, in words in Public Sans, with its symbol in the serif where the page gives it one ("rank r", "uses of “worship” x₁"). A bar chart names what its bars measure, and a chart whose bars point two ways names each way (Joaquín's rule).
- Snap draggable points to integers so readouts stay clean.
- Build figures with the helpers of `plane.js` (listed in `plane-js.md`): `createPlane` for a 2D plane (for small, readable cross products, a small one, 0 to 10, as the homogeneous coordinates note uses), `createSpace` for 3D, `logChart` for log–log axes, `stepLabels` for the labels of a plane stepped through with Back and Next, `makeHandle`, `modeButtons`, `roving`, `steadyHeight`, `wholeNumberInput`, `localNum`, and its matrix, label and geometry helpers.
- To let readers pick a mode (a kind of transformation, an interpolation method), use a `.modes` group of buttons with `data-mode` and `aria-pressed`, wired with `modeButtons`.
- In a figure made of words (HTML rather than SVG, such as a list of words or a tagged sentence), each item the reader can pick is a button: the arrow keys move between them, the item picked is outlined, and Tab enters the list at it (`roving`).
- A panel whose content changes with its mode keeps the height of its tallest mode (`steadyHeight`), so the page below never jumps.
- Gray values of images are drawn with `color-mix()` between `--img-lo` (0) and `--img-hi` (255), so bright pixels stay bright in both themes. Mark pixels with no value explicitly (the warping figure crosses them out), since an empty cell looks like black or white.
- To let readers edit a vector, put number inputs inside a `.col.coef` bracket, as the line-as-vector figure does. Accept whole numbers only, and leave the figure unchanged while a field holds something else (a lone minus sign, say), as `wholeNumberInput` does; single numbers such as angles use a `.coef` span without `.col`.
- Keep readouts light: one key calculation with the current numbers, the one the section is about, shown as a calculation rather than only its result, and one short sentence saying what it means. The sentence carries the one or two numbers the reader needs. Leave out checks, simplified forms, extra divisions and second formulas, or give their result in the sentence; explanations belong in the section's text. A degenerate case gets one short sentence.
- Handle degenerate cases with a clear message: coincident points, parallel lines (`r = 0`), coincident lines, points outside the view.
- Use `≈` instead of `=` when a displayed value is rounded.
- Every draggable element also moves with the arrow keys and has an `aria-label` with its current position.
- Interactive SVGs use `role="group"` with an `aria-label`, not `role="img"`: an image's contents are hidden from screen readers, which would hide the handles.
- Create handles with `plane.js`, which enlarges their touch area on touch screens to about 44px. Check each figure at 390px and 320px wide.
- In 3D figures, keep `stretch` at 1 whenever an angle matters, such as a perpendicular vector; a larger `stretch` exaggerates the vertical axis and is fine when only lines, planes and where they meet matter. Draw fixed geometry with the space's `line`, `poly`, `dot` and `pin` so it follows the view, draw what lies below a translucent plane before it and what lies above after it, and put fixed labels in a top layer.
- Labels that move with a view the reader turns must not jump from one side of their mark to the other: place them by rules that change smoothly with the view, such as fixed screen offsets. For anything the reader turns or drags, prefer `placeBeside`, which keeps a label on the side it had, to `placeClear`, which picks among eight directions each time.
- A figure responds smoothly: a small drag moves what it draws a little, never making points or fits jump. Where it makes a discrete choice from continuous input (an inlier set, a threshold, a winning sample, a mode picked automatically), a tiny move can flip the choice back and forth, so simulate dragging every handle in small steps (a few data units, or one screen pixel) before handing it over, in Node against the figure's own math and in the browser with Playwright, and count jumps and back-and-forth flips. Prefer deterministic rules that change state once along a drag (an exhaustive search with a clear tie-break) over rules that depend on noisy intermediate fits; drawn at a small scale, a few screen pixels are many data units.
- Before showing a figure whose labels name points, script a sweep of every state (each point chosen, the handle over a grid of positions, each mode and view) and check that each label is present, wholly inside the frame, off the axis lines and clear of other text, and that ties and points shared by several items show as such: a label for each tied point, "+N" for items sharing one, the readout naming the tie. A label that vanishes or a tie shown as a single winner reads as a bug or a false answer, and Joaquín tests exactly these states.
- Name each handle or point the reader moves in plain terms where the figure is introduced, and phrase the readout in those terms rather than in geometry alone (a query point is a query that says one word six times and another twice). Check that the default view shows the figure's key difference at a visible scale; where the data crowds, start in a close-up and offer the whole view. The reader meets the handle before any explanation of it, and a difference too small to see teaches nothing.
- Keep a figure's controls few and uniform: one kind of control where possible, a few well-chosen presets on `.modes` buttons rather than several handles with different meanings, and no secondary interactions (picking items for details) unless the section is about them, since each kind is one more thing to learn before the idea shows. When Joaquín calls a figure complex, list what the reader has to learn and decode, and offer concrete simpler versions.
- A figure's options (a display mode, the sound, a test variant in a mockup) are controls inside that figure, near its other controls, such as a gear at the end of its control row that opens a menu for that figure only; never a bar for the whole page, which hides where its effect lands, and never over the drawing, where they hide values.
- Every class in a figure gets a solid color from the tokens; a third class beside carmine and ink takes `--ok` green, never an outline, which reads as missing or disabled. Record each class-to-color choice in the course's rules file. A mark in one class's color never sits beside a word or label that names the other class or pushes toward it: check every visible label against the color beside it, prefer examples whose words agree with their color even if they are not the strongest, and explain a counterintuitive case in the text before the reader meets it. A reader reads a label's meaning before a figure's logic, so a color that contradicts it looks like an error.
- In a color scale, the strongest color goes to what is preferred or what the reader should look at, and bad or costly values stay faint, since the eye takes the strongest color as the thing to pick. Don't fix a scale by inverting it where values start optimistic.
- Before changing a file that several figures share (`plane.js`, `notes.css`, a course's figure module), take press-by-press snapshots of every published figure, and compare them after the change (`tools/snapall.mjs` in `course-materials/natural-language-processing/handoff-note5/` covers all three courses).
