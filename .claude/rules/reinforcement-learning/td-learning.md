# The TD note: its figures, runs and numbers

Its text's example, the wandering home, is in `wandering-home.md`.

- **Section 1: the TD(0) figure.** It predicts values for a fixed random policy, and its presses are choose (the policy's pick, in yellow), take, the TD error with its target, update; after move 1, choosing and taking share a press. TD(0) prediction for the random policy (each move ¼) on the cookie grid, γ = 0.9, α = 0.5, every V at 0, seed 15395, the same 30 episodes as the Monte Carlo prediction figure, 702 moves. Move 1 (A1 up) has δ = 0. Episode 1 takes 5 moves (A1 up, A2 left into the wall, A2 right, B2 right, C2 up), and only its last TD error is not 0: V(C2) 0 → 0.5. In episode 2, B2 → C2 targets 0.9 · 0.5 = 0.45 and V(B2) → 0.23; the next move, C2 → C1, has δ = −0.5 and V(C2) → 0.25. At move 83, in episode 5, A3 is the last estimate to leave 0, borrowing from A2; no estimate returns to 0 after that (the lowest is 0.017), since each update keeps at least half of it. After 30 episodes the estimates (0.37 0.71 · / 0.33 0.65 0.86 / 0.27 0.28 0.51, top row first) keep every strict ordering of v_π, but miss it by up to 0.35, in C2: 0.86 against 0.51, where the Monte Carlo averages of the same episodes miss by at most 0.13. The route the estimates lead along is A1 A2 B2 C2 C3.
- **Section 4: the batch figure.** It steps through passes over a fixed batch instead of moves: each of its first passes has a press for the sums of the increments, computed with the table held fixed (each cell's TD errors, or each visit's return minus the estimate), and one for the update, which outlines every estimate it changes in green; then Next skips passes. Nothing acts there, so the agent stands in A1 throughout (Joaquín's choice, after a mockup without it). Batch TD(0) and batch Monte Carlo, behind mode buttons, replay the 702 moves of the shared 30 episodes (seed 15395), γ = 0.9, every V at 0, α = 0.005: small because each estimate moves by α times a sum, and 176 of the moves start in A1, the most; with it no estimate overshoots, and in both runs no estimate ever drops. Batch Monte Carlo counts every visit, as the book's batch updating (6.3) and the TD note's constant-α Monte Carlo do (Joaquín's choice over the Monte Carlo figure's first-visit averages: those, 0.13 from v_π, would have lost to batch TD(0) on this batch, while every visit wins here). Batch TD(0), 5 slides and 7 presses: pass 1, only the 30 moves into the cookie have a TD error, 1, 15 from B3 and 15 from C2 (the TD errors of C2's 44 moves add up to 15), so V(C2) ← 0 + 0.005 · 15 = 0.075, shown as 0.08; pass 2, the targets of moves into B3 and C2 borrow their estimates (red), 32 of B2's 73 moves go there, Σδ = 32 · (0 + 0.9 · 0.075 − 0) = 2.16, V(B2) ≈ 0.01, and A3 and C1 leave 0 too. Each cell leaves 0 at the pass of its distance from the cookie (A2 and B1 at 3, A1 at 4), but below 0.005 until passes 4 to 6, so the figure skips from pass 2 to pass 147: after pass 146 no estimate changes at two decimals (A2 last, its limit just above 0.275). It ends on the values of the model the moves suggest, 0.36 0.54 · / 0.28 0.37 0.63 / 0.23 0.28 0.39; C2's 44 moves went 15 into the cookie, 12 into the wall, 7 to C1 and 10 to B2, and (15 · 1 + 0.9 · (12 · 0.63 + 7 · 0.39 + 10 · 0.37))/44 ≈ 0.63. The largest miss from v_π is 0.11, in C2 (0.63 against 0.51), all of it from C2's own moves: with them a quarter each, C2's value is 0.512, and the cookie's share alone (15 of 44, against 11) accounts for about two thirds. Online TD(0) with α = 0.5 had ended at 0.86 there. Route A1 B1 C1 C2 C3. Batch Monte Carlo, 4 slides and 5 presses: pass 1, A1's 176 returns add up to 38.47, V(A1) ≈ 0.19, and all 8 estimates change at once; after pass 17 nothing changes at two decimals (the slide is pass 18; C2, with the fewest visits, 44, settles last). It ends on the every-visit averages, 0.36 0.49 · / 0.26 0.32 0.55 / 0.22 0.27 0.41, C2's 24.34/44 ≈ 0.55, at most 0.10 from v_π, in C1, a little less than batch TD(0)'s 0.11; batch TD(0) ends farthest from them in C2 (0.63 against 0.55; the limits differ by 0.07). Over 10,000 other batches of 30 episodes (seeds 1 to 10,000), batch TD(0)'s largest miss is the smaller in 7,908 (79%; by root mean square error, 84%), which the last slide calls about 4 times in 5. Same route.
- **Section 5: the Sarsa figure.** One-step Sarsa on the cliff, α = 0.5, γ = 1, ε = 0.1, seed 557070, 30 episodes, 860 moves. Episode 1, played move by move: 14 moves. A1 down into the wall twice (A′ picks down again, so the same pair is updated twice, to −0.5 and then −0.75), A1 left into the wall, then the first fall, move 4, right from A1; A′ is chosen before the update, while right is still worth 0, so move 5 falls the same way (Q(A1, right): 0 → −50 → −75); then up, picked at random, and through row 3 to the goal (A2 B2 C2 C3 D3 E3 F3 F2 F1). The best moves first reach the goal in episode 9, in 9 moves through C3 and D3, and first run along the edge in episode 16; at move 722, in episode 24, the agent takes right from A2, picks down in B2 at random as A′ (worth −50.44, after one earlier fall), and Q(A2, right) drops from −5.8 to −28.62, below up (−5.88), for good. At the end the route runs one row up, values −8.04, −7.12, −6.27, −5.46, −4.69, −3.91, −2.96, −2, −1; in A2 up −7.12 and right −28.62; 8 falls, the last in episode 28. The text gives the limits with a small step size, q_π(A2, up) = −9.39 and q_π(A2, right) = −12.01.
- One-step Sarsa with α = 0.5 does not settle on a route within 30 episodes (8 of 60,000 seeds do by episode 22), since its target uses the random next move and the open cells' values stay within a few tenths of each other; its figure builds on what does hold, a fall picked as A′ pushing Q(A2, right) below Q(A2, up) for good.
- **Pseudocode beside the TD(0) figure** ("Tabular TD(0)"), numbered as the panel shows it: 1 V(s) ← 0, for every s (plain); head "Repeat, for each episode:"; 2 S ← the first state (plain); head "Repeat, until S is terminal:"; 3 A ← the action π gives for S (pick); 4 Take A; observe R, S′ (take); 5 δ ← R + γV(S′) − V(S) (target); 6 V(S) ← V(S) + αδ (update); 7 S ← S′ (plain). The first slide plays 1; move 1's presses play 2 and 3, then 4, 5 and 6; a press that chooses and takes plays 7 (or 2 at an episode's start), 3, 4, and a press that also gives the TD error of 0 goes on to 5 and ends there with no marks, as before the panel (its readout shows the TD error), each line drawn as it lights (phases from the one-line builds; choosing and taking animate, and the take holds its line 300 ms for the glide, 600 ms onto the cookie so that it is eaten, and a wall hit stays marked); episode 2's first press plays 2 and 3; "Every cell reached" plays 6, the last slide nothing, and a skip one pass of 3 to 7 per move. The updates whose text starts "Still at t = …" say "At t = …" alone in the readout (4 short texts).
- **Pseudocode beside the batch figure**, one per mode, given through `show(slides, frame, code)`: 1 V(s) ← 0, for every s (plain); head "Repeat, for each pass, until V stops changing:"; head "With V held fixed, for each step t of the batch:"; 2 δ_t ← R_{t+1} + γV(S_{t+1}) − V(S_t) for batch TD(0), "The increment G_t − V(S_t)" for batch Monte Carlo (target); 3 For each s: V(s) ← V(s) + α Σ δ_t (or Σ (G_t − V(S_t))), over the steps from s (update). The first slide plays 1, each pass's sums press 2 and its update press 3, the settled slide after a skip 3, the last slide nothing; a skip runs 2, 3 once per pass. Pass 1's batch TD(0) update has a short text that leaves out the sum line 3 writes but keeps "Each estimate changes once". Pending shared change: with the panel, the mode buttons need a grid area of their own, which `notes.css` does not have yet (without it, the mode buttons take a column: on phones the grid disappears, and at desktop width the panel is narrow); the rule, checked by injection at 1280, 390 and 320 px: `.fig.steps.with-code:has(> .modes) { grid-template-areas: "modes" "draw" "ctl" "code" "ro" "foot"; }` and, from 840 px, `grid-template-rows: auto auto auto auto 1fr; grid-template-areas: "modes code" "draw code" "draw ro" "ctl ro" "foot ro";`.
- **Pseudocode beside the Sarsa figure** ("Sarsa"): 1 Q(s, a) ← 0, for every s, a (plain); head "Repeat, for each episode:"; 2 S ← the first state (plain); 3 A ← ε-greedy in S, from Q (pick); head "Repeat, until S is terminal:"; 4 Take A; observe R, S′ (take); 5 A′ ← ε-greedy in S′, from Q (pick); 6 target ← R + γQ(S′, A′) (target); 7 Q(S, A) ← Q(S, A) + α[target − Q(S, A)] (update); 8 S ← S′, A ← A′ (plain). The first slide plays 1; move 1 plays 2 and 3, then 4, 5, 6, 7; each later take press of episode 1 plays 8 and 4 (the yellow A, then the move), and the other presses 5, 6 and 7; a whole move plays 8, 4, 5, 6, 7 (without 5 at the goal), each line drawn as it lights (taking and choosing A′ animate; the take holds its line 300 ms, so that the glide or fall plays out, and after a wall hit the later lines keep the wall marked, drawn without a second nudge); "Along the edge" plays 8, so the next press, the take of "The edge pays for exploring", plays 4 alone; the last slide plays nothing, and a skip one pass of 4 to 8 per move. Short texts: the updates whose text starts "Still at t = …" say "At t = …" (6), the pull's update says "toward the target" for "toward it", and the pull's target names the move it uses (B2 down, the step into the cliff) instead of "that move".
