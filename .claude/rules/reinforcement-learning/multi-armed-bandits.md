# The multi-armed bandits note: its world, runs and numbers

Note 1 of the course (book ch. 2: intro, §2.1–2.6 and §2.8; §2.7, UCB, gets one sentence and no formula). Its plan is the "Note 1" part of `course-materials/reinforcement-learning/reference/plan-notes-1-3-2026-10-20.md`. The page uses the book's R_t after A_t without comment; the MDP note explains the switch to R_{t+1}. It shows no book equation numbers, as the other chapter notes do not.

## The world: the angler's lake

- Four spots, pier, reeds, rocks and bridge (Spanish: muelle, juncos, rocas, puente). Each morning the angler fishes one, and the catch is a Poisson draw with mean q\* = 1, 3, 4 and 2 fish; the rocks are best. In section 3 the water warms at morning 151: the rocks drop to 1 and the reeds rise to 5 (`QSTAR_WARM`).
- Spanish page: the angler is la pescadora, a catch una pesca, a morning una mañana, a season una temporada, the warming "el agua se entibia"; the title and module are "Bandits de múltiples brazos", and the weights figure's x-axis reads "el inicio y las pescas, la más antigua primero" (a longer label ran out of the frame at 390 px).
- Catches are whole numbers ≥ 0, which is what traps a greedy angler starting from 0 (section 1) and what makes the missing baseline hurt (section 5).
- None of it is the book's testbed, the study guide's exercises or Exercise 2.5's random walk.

## Scripts

All in `course-materials/reinforcement-learning/scripts/notes-1-3/bandits/` (not in the repository):
- `world.mjs`: the spots, means, warming, `rngFrom` (mulberry32), `poisson`, `argmaxRandom` (random tie-break), `softmax`.
- `season.mjs`: one season (sample averages or constant α, greedy or ε-greedy, or the gradient bandit with the baseline the average of the catches before t, R̄₁ = R₁). Note that it draws one random number for the ε test even when ε = 0, which the seeds below depend on.
- `note1-numbers.mjs`: prints every number the page quotes, in page order (output kept in `note1-numbers.txt`). Rerun it after any change and compare.
- `note1-figures.mjs`: draws the six static figures from the same runs, with English and Spanish labels, and with `--write` puts each SVG inside its `figure.diagram` (`#fig-lake`, `#fig-greedy`, `#fig-weights`, `#fig-warming`, `#fig-optimistic`, `#fig-probs`) on both pages (the Spanish one when it exists). Captions and text are written by hand and must match the numbers.

Seeds: every 1,000-season simulation (300 mornings each) restarts from seed 101, so one setting gives one number everywhere; the windows are named by the mornings they average, inclusive. Section 1's one season is seed 19 (greedy from 0), section 4's seed 7 (greedy from 8).

## Numbers the page quotes

- **Section 1.** k = 4, ε = 0.2: greedy spot 0.85, each other 0.05; two tied, 0.45 each and 0.05 for the other two. Seed 19, greedy from 0: the reeds win the first tie and give 5, then 2, 1, 3, 1, 4, 0, 3 (estimates 5, 3.5, 2.67, 2.75, 2.4, 2.67, 2.29, 2.38), and the season never leaves the reeds in 300 mornings. 1,000 seasons: greedy never leaves its first spot in 90% ("9 in 10") (the other 10% caught nothing on their first morning) and is at the rocks on about 29% of mornings all season (25% on morning 1, a four-way tie, then 29% from the first window, 1–10, on; the page says "about 29% … all season long"); fish per morning at mornings 291–300: greedy 2.61, ε = 0.05 3.82, ε = 0.2 3.67; with exact estimates greedy 4 (the best possible, the rocks every morning), ε = 0.05 3.925 and ε = 0.2 3.7, written as these exact values. Rocks share at 91–100: 76% (ε = 0.2) and 62% (ε = 0.05); at 291–300 83% and 84% (not on the page: the fish counts carry the comparison).
- **Section 2.** The six mornings: reeds 2, rocks 5, rocks 3, pier 1, rocks 6, rocks 2; the rocks' pulls n = 1–4 at t = 2, 3, 5, 6; Q(rocks) 0 → 5 → 4 → 4.67 → 4, the plain average of 5, 3, 6, 2.
- **Section 3.** α = 0.25 from Q₁ = 0 on 5, 3, 6, 2: 1.25, 1.69, 2.77, 2.57. Weights of R₁…R₄ 0.105, 0.141, 0.188, 0.25 and of Q₁ 0.316, summing to 1; Q₁'s weight falls under 0.05 after 11 catches. The warming picture (150 catches of exactly 4, then exactly 1, the rocks fished every morning): after 1, 5, 10, 30 mornings, sample average 3.98, 3.90, 3.81, 3.50 and α = 0.25 3.25, 1.71, 1.17, 1.00. 1,000 seasons with the warming, ε = 0.2: best-spot share, sample average against α = 0.25, 81%/76% at 141–150, 15%/81% at 161–170, 48%/84% at 191–200; fish per morning 151–300 3.46 and 4.34. Step-size conditions: Σ 1/n to 1,000 = 7.49; Σ 1/n² stays below 1.645 (π²/6 is not written, since π is the policy here).
- **Section 4.** From Q₁ = 8 with α = 0.25, the catches 5, 3, 6, 2 give 5.11, of which 2.53 is the start. Greedy from 8 with sample averages tries every spot about once: a first catch of 8 or more (P(Poisson(4) ≥ 8) ≈ 0.05) keeps a spot in the running, and in 2.6% of the 1,000 seasons the first four mornings do not visit four spots (`check-facts.mjs`); hence "about one try" and "almost every catch disappoints". With a constant α the trap of section 1 holds too (a catch of 0 multiplies the estimate by 1 − α, never reaching 0). Seed 7, greedy from 8, sample averages: pier 2, rocks 4, bridge 1, reeds 4, reeds 3 (a tie at 4 with the rocks), then the rocks for the remaining 295 mornings. Rocks share at 291–300: greedy from 0 29%, greedy from 8 with 1/n 78%, greedy from 8 with α = 0.25 93%, ε = 0.05 from 0 with α = 0.25 77%.
- **Section 5.** Softmax of 0.3, −0.1, 0.5, 0 is 0.275, 0.185, 0.336, 0.204, unchanged with 5 added. The six mornings replayed with α = 0.1, H from 0: morning 1 changes nothing; morning 5 (6 against R̄ = 2.75) takes π(rocks) from 0.319 to 0.387; morning 6 (2 against R̄ = 3.4) brings it to 0.360, and with no baseline on morning 6 alone (same H before) the same catch would have raised it to 0.426 (dropping it on all six mornings would give 0.543, not quoted); Σ H = 0 throughout. The figure's probabilities: before morning 5 0.193, 0.244, 0.319, 0.244; after 5 0.176, 0.219, 0.387, 0.219; after 6 0.183, 0.229, 0.360, 0.229. 1,000 seasons: rocks at 291–300 98% with the baseline, 86% without.
