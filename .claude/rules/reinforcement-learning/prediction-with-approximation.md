# The prediction with approximation note: its figures and numbers

- The note is on the cart-pole alone, with no example of its own (Joaquín's decision). The agent sees only the pole's angle, in six ranges of 4° (state aggregation), and its weights are drawn as bars, one per range, on an axis of the steps until the pole falls, with a small cart and pole under each range at its middle angle (`angleChart`); the true values of visited states are red dots, and each range's long-run average a short red line. Joaquín chose this after a first mockup that cut the plane of the pole's angle and angular velocity into 6 × 6 cells, each showing its weight, which he found unclear in showing what the agent learned: with bars, what the agent learns reads at once (about 19 steps until the fall within 4° of upright, 12 from 4° to 8°, 5 past 8°), and leaving out the angular velocity makes the lessons of sections 2 and 5 concrete (the spread inside a range, and TD's bias, since the next range depends on which way the pole moves).
- The slide figures pass `createCartPole(svg)` to `slideshow()` as its `view`. Episode 1 is played step by step, its first push in two presses (choose, take); gradient Monte Carlo then updates its steps in order, t = 0 to T − 1, as the book's algorithm does, one press each, and semi-gradient TD(0) shows its first step in four presses (choose, take, the TD error, the update). Later slides sum up episodes: the weights, and the pole as it last fell, without the angle dot. α shrinks from one episode to the next, 0.1 · 20/(k + 19) in episode k: with α constant, a single long episode kept gradient Monte Carlo's bars 2 to 8 steps off, even at 0.002, and the last slide could not show what the agent had learned.
- **The numbers (text and figures).** The random policy; the agent's one feature is the pole's angle in six ranges of 4° from −12° to 12°. References from 400,000 episodes (seed 424242), left and right made symmetric: time per range 8.2%, 12.4% and 29.4% from the outer ranges to the middle ones (85.3% of steps stay in their range; an episode lasts 22.3 steps on average); each range's average return, where gradient Monte Carlo settles, 4.76, 11.81 and 18.90 steps; the variance of the true values inside each range 28.40, 37.15 and 15.19, so the least VE is 22.79 (a typical miss of 4.8 steps); TD(0) settles at 3.91, 10.70 and 22.28 (VE 29.93), n-step TD at 3.46, 10.12, 21.78 (n = 2), 3.49, 10.05, 20.78 (n = 4) and 4.08, 10.85, 19.55 (n = 8). Inside the range 4° to 8°, true values run from about 2 to 23 steps. Section 2's figure starts every bar at 12 (VE ≈ 59.4) over 500 of 1,000 states drawn from the visits of 50,000 episodes (seed 777), each valued by 400 rollouts (`rl/cp-dots.json`); its VE is 22.8 plus Σ μ(w − average)², so the dots only illustrate. The slide figures: seed 214, 2,000 episodes, α = 0.1 · 20/(k + 19). Episode 1 takes 8 steps: the pole starts 2.8° to the right, the policy pushes left six times, right once, left once, and the pole falls to the right at 12.5°, through the ranges 0° to 4° four times (the fourth state at 3.97°, "just under 4°"), 4° to 8° twice and 8° to 12° twice. Gradient Monte Carlo's updates: 0.8, 1.42, 1.88, 2.19, then 0.4, 0.66, then 0.2, 0.28; TD(0)'s: 0.1, 0.2, 0.3 in the start range (δ = 1 while the pole stays in it), 0.37 when it leaves, then 0.1, 0.19, 0.1, 0.19. Episode 2 takes 20 steps, its first 11 in −4° to 0°, and the pole falls to the left; gradient Monte Carlo's weight there becomes 9.4. The two middle ranges average, for TD(0) and gradient Monte Carlo: 3.9 and 21.0 after 10 episodes, 14.5 and 20.2 after 100, 19.0 and 18.1 after 500. After 2,000 episodes gradient Monte Carlo's bars are 5.2, 12.4, 18.3, 18.3, 11.9, 4.8 (within 0.6 of the averages) and TD(0)'s 3.9, 10.3, 21.0, 20.9, 10.2, 3.8, on their way to 22.3 in the middle. Computed by `rl/cp-angle-data.mjs` and `rl/cp-angle-slides.mjs` in the fbc772b2 scratchpad.
