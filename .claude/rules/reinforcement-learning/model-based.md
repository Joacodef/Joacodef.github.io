# The model-based note: its decisions, figures and numbers

- Joaquín's decisions: the R-Max and Dyna-Q figures (sections 2 and 3) are on the cookie grid; value iteration is explained in one paragraph of section 1, since no earlier note does; section 4, on a model gone wrong, is short and stops where the chapter file stops, before Dyna-Q+; comparisons drawn from the figures' worlds stay in the figures' slides, not in the text. The tables of sections 1 and 2, the corridor figure and the diagram of section 4 were added to break up walls of text.
- Presses: Q-planning's are pick a state and a move, the model's answer, the update, then one press per planning step. R-Max's follow the slides' loop: choose, take, count, then value iteration on the new model, which is also the first line of the next move, outlining every value it changes in green. Dyna-Q's are choose, take, the Q-learning update, the model, and planning, whose steps after the cookie get a slide each. The faint copy of the agent plays every step of the Q-planning figure, and the Dyna-Q planning steps that have a slide of their own.
- **The robot's home as a model (text).** The TD note's five batch episodes, counted: hallway → study tried once (1 of 1, −1), charging in the study tried five times (+1 three times, the charger free; 0 twice, taken), and a door from the hallway to the garage never tried; γ = 1. A distribution model of charging: +1 with 0.6, 0 with 0.4; a night of ten trips has 2^10 = 1,024 sequences of free and taken. Value iteration, the garage aside: V(study) = 0.6, V(hallway) = −1 + 0.6 = −0.4, batch TD(0)'s values; a table gives the sweeps, each from the values of the sweep before: (study, hallway) = (0, 0), (0.6, −1), (0.6, −0.4), then no change, the news reaching the hallway one sweep after the study. Q-planning with α = 1/n on the sample answers +1, 0, +1, +1, 0: 1, 0.5, 0.67, 0.75, 0.6, in a table of n, R and Q(study, charge). R-Max with R_max = 1 (a free charger): the untried garage door is worth 1 against −0.4 through the study; behind it a charger free every time, so once known the garage is worth −1 + 1 = 0; an untried door at the end of a two-move corridor would be worth −1 − 1 + 1 = −1, not worth a look. k: a charger free 3 times in 5 is taken on a single visit 2 times in 5; all k visits find it taken (2/5)^k of the time, in a table: 0.4, 0.16, 0.064, 0.0256, 0.01024 for k = 1 to 5. A model gone wrong: after a hundred trips through the garage door, R-Max values the locked door at −m/100 after m bumps while it still picks it (−0.4 at m = 40, a tie with the study), and the study wins from the 41st bump (the door then at −0.407); a back door found locked once and later opened is retried by Dyna-Q only by a random move, ε/3 = one visit to the hallway in 30, and by R-Max never.
- **Section 1: the Q-planning figure.** The cookie grid with a model that knows all 32 moves, γ = 0.9, α = 1 (a move always does the same thing, so there is nothing to average, and each step sets its value to the target), every value from 0 and shown; each step picks one of the 8 states and one of the 4 moves at random (one random number each); the agent stands in A1 throughout, and the faint copy plays every step. Seed 5659: steps 1 to 3 pick A2 right, B2 up and B3 right, a way from A2 to the cookie in walking order, so only step 3 changes a value (B3 right 1); step 9 picks B2 up again, now 0 + 0.9 · 1 = 0.9; step 30 gives A1 up 0.73 (0 + 0.9 · 0.81) and a shortest route, A1 A2 B2 B3 C3, while 24 values are still 0; step 86 raises B1 right from 0.66 to 0.81 (set at step 61, when C1's best was 0.73; C1 up reached 0.9 at step 66); from step 95 no step can change any value (0.73, 0.81, 0.9 and 1 along every shortest route), and 34 of the 95 steps changed one. 10 presses. Across 3,000 seeds: the first change at a median step of 12, A1's first value at 65, every value optimal at 189, about 33 steps that change anything (not quoted in the note).
- **Section 2: the corridor figure ("worth a look?").** Not a slideshow: the robot's home as in the opening diagram, with the garage door, never tried, at the end of a corridor of d known moves (−1 each), which the reader drags from d = 0 to 3 (arrow keys too). R-Max values it at −d + R_max = 1, 0, −1, −2 against the study's −1 + 0.6 = −0.4, so it goes to look up to d = 1; the way it does not take fades. The readout: the calculation, compared with −0.4, and one sentence. Once known, with a charger free every time, the garage is worth −d from the hallway, worth it only at d = 0 (not shown; the text gives d = 0). Computed by `rl/mb-walls.mjs` in the e6e713fb scratchpad.
- **Section 2: the R-Max figure.** The cookie grid, k = 1, R_max = 1, γ = 0.9, greedy on the values value iteration gives (ties broken at random, one random number per choice), seed 210, 5 episodes of 8, 21, 13, 4 and 4 moves (50 in all). Episode 1, played move by move, moves 1 to 3 line by line (choose, take, count, value iteration): A1 left (into the wall), A1 up, A2 up, A3 right, B3 down, B2 up, B3 up (into the wall), B3 right (the cookie), every choice a tie among untried moves worth 1; each move once known is worth 0 + 0.9 · 1 = 0.9, and the cookie's move 1 for real. Episode 2's first move: the known route A1 up, A2 up, A3 right, B3 right is worth 0.9^3 ≈ 0.73, but R-Max values up at 0.9 (A2 still has untried moves) and picks right, untried, in a tie with down. The last untried move, C2 up, comes at move 42 (episode 3, move 13); every value is then optimal, 0.73, 0.81, 0.9 and 1 along every shortest route, and episodes 4 and 5 take 4 moves. 21 presses. Across seeds R-Max's first episode on the cookie grid has a median of 17 moves; on the cliff (R_max = −1, γ = 1) a median of 92, and it falls in exactly 5 times in all 400 seeds tried, once from each edge cell, and never again (not quoted in the note).
- **Section 3: the Dyna-Q figure.** The cookie grid, α = 0.5, ε = 0.1, γ = 0.9, n = 10 planning steps after every move, each on a random state seen before and a random action taken there, one random stream for acting and planning, seed 6350, 30 episodes, 134 moves. Episode 1, played move by move (move 1 line by line: choose, take, update, model, planning): A1 up, A2 right, B2 down, B1 left, A1 right, B1 up (random), B2 up, B3 right; planning changes nothing until the cookie. At the cookie the update gives B3 right 0.5, and each of the ten planning steps has a slide, played by the faint copy of the agent from the state replayed to where the model says the move leads: B2 up 0 → 0.23, A2 right 0 → 0.1, B3 right 0.5 → 0.75, B1 up 0 → 0.1, B2 down 0 → 0.05, B3 right → 0.88 → 0.94, A2 right → 0.15, A1 up 0 → 0.07 (the ninth: the route A1 A2 B2 B3 C3 appears, the walk's moves 1, 2, 7 and 8 with its loop cut out), B1 up → 0.15; Q-learning alone would have one value. Episode 2 takes the route, 4 moves, and the best moves keep a shortest route to the end. Final values along it 0.73, 0.81, 0.9, 1; only 15 of the 32 moves tried, and after episode 1 every new move came from a random move or from a cell a random move had led to. 31 presses. Over 2,000 seeds (with planning on its own random stream), the best moves settle on a shortest route in a median episode of 5 for Q-learning alone, 2 for n = 5 or 10, and 1 for n = 50 (not quoted in the note).
- **Section 4: the wall figure.** The cliff, γ = 1, 30 episodes, a wall between A2 and B2 in episodes 11 to 20; its first press plays episodes 1 to 10 quickly, and mode buttons switch between the two runs. A skip over a run of hits says so ("The agent hits the wall again and again, and stays in A2"). R-Max (seed 12, k = 1, R_max = −1, the largest reward on the cliff): its first episode tries every move, 100 moves with 5 falls, and episodes 2 to 10 take the edge, 7 moves; it has gone right from A2 14 times. In episode 11 it bumps into the wall 29 times in a row: while right is best, its value is −6 − m/14 after m bumps (the model expects (14 + m)/14 tries to get through), and each bump lowers 13 values by the same 0.07 (right, down and left in A2, A3 down, B2 left, A1's four moves, and the falls from B2 to E2, which lead back to A1), outlined in green; after 28 bumps right and up tie at −8 (3 tries expected, 2 moves lost, as many as going around adds), a random tie-break takes the wall once more, and right falls to −8.02, the only value that changes; episode 11 takes 38 moves, every later trip 9, and after the wall comes down R-Max never tries right in A2 again (its values never change after episode 12; its model says right gets through 14 times in 43). Dyna-Q (seed 92461, α = 0.5, ε = 0.1, n = 10): episodes 1 to 10 take 94, 51, 7, 9, 9, 11, 7, 9, 9 and 12 moves (7 falls in episode 1, 1 in episode 2), its best moves follow the edge from episode 6, and its model holds all 76 moves; in episode 11 it bumps 3 times, each lowering right by half a move (−5.98, −6.48, −6.98, −7.48), below up's −6.98, and goes around in 12 moves, never hitting the wall again; after the wall comes down its model values right at −9 against up's −8, and a random move takes right in A2 in episode 25 (the wall gone for 4 episodes): the update raises it from −9 to −7.5 (target −1 + (−5)), above up, and from episode 25 every trip takes the edge, 7 moves. Across seeds (not quoted): R-Max knowing every move bumps 2n or 2n + 1 times for n passes; Dyna-Q's edge after 10 episodes in 286 of 300 seeds, about 3 bumps, and a first random retry a median of 18 episodes after the wall comes down.
