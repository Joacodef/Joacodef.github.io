# The Monte Carlo note: its runs and numbers

Its text's example, the wandering home, is in `wandering-home.md`.

- **Section 1: the prediction figure.** First-visit Monte Carlo prediction for the random policy (each move ¼), every V at 0, seed 15395 (the TD(0) figure's, so the two learn from the same episodes), 30 episodes, 702 moves. Episode 1: 5 moves, A1 up, A2 left into the wall, A2 right, B2 right, C2 up; its sweep gives C2 1, B2 0.9, skips A2's 0.81 at t = 2 and gives A2 0.73 from its first visit, and A1 0.66. Episode 2: 7 moves, A1 right, B1 up, B2 right, C2 down, C1 down into the wall, C1 up, C2 up; its sweep skips C2 at t = 6 and C1 at t = 5, gives C1 0.81, and gives C2 its second return, 0.73, for an average of (1 + 0.73)/2 ≈ 0.86. Episode 3 takes 41 moves: A1's return is about 0.015, and its average falls from 0.59 to 0.40. After 30 episodes the averages are 0.37 0.55 · / 0.34 0.45 0.62 / 0.27 0.26 0.44, all above v_π and at most 0.13 from it, in C1, which has the fewest returns (15; A1 has 30); they swap the order of A1 and B1. The route the estimates lead along is A1 A2 B2 C2 C3, the shortest. The TD note's batch figure replays these 30 episodes; its batch Monte Carlo counts every visit, so it ends on other averages than these first-visit ones.
- **Section 4: the control figure.** On-policy first-visit Monte Carlo control with ε = 0.1, values from 0, seed 28869, 30 episodes, 148 moves. Episode 1: 8 moves, A1 up, A2 right, B2 down, B1 right, C1 left, B1 right (random), C1 up, C2 up; its sweep gives returns 1, 0.9, (0.81 skipped: B1 right was first made at t = 3), 0.73, 0.66, 0.59, 0.53, 0.48, and the best moves then cut out the loop (in C1, up 0.9 beats left 0.73): 6 moves. Episode 2 averages Q(B1, right) to (0.66 + 0.81)/2 = 0.73. In episode 9 a random first move, right from A1, returns 0.73, above Q(A1, up) = 0.53, and the route becomes the 4-move A1 B1 C1 C2 C3 for good. At the end: 0.73, 0.79, 0.89, 1 along the route (the shortest trip's returns are 0.73, 0.81, 0.9, 1), Q(A1, up) = 0.54, and A3 and B3 never visited.
- **The prediction figure's pseudocode** (titled "First-visit Monte Carlo prediction"), from the note's list of steps, numbered 1 to 6 by the panel: 1 V(s) ← 0 and no returns, for every state (plain); "Repeat, for each episode:" and "For t = 0, 1, … until the cookie:" (heads); 2 A_t ← the move π picks in S_t (pick); 3 Take A_t; observe R_{t+1}, S_{t+1} (take); 4 G ← 0 (plain); "For t = T − 1, …, 0:" (head); 5 G ← γG + R_{t+1} (target); "If S_t does not appear earlier in the episode:" (head); 6 Add G to the returns of S_t; V(S_t) ← their average (update). The first slide lights 1; move 1's two presses light 2 and 3; every later move plays 2 then 3 in phases (the pick in yellow, then the move, line 3 held three line times so that the glide and the cookie eaten finish before the engine's last, unanimated drawing), and the move into the cookie adds 4. In the sweep, a press that only computes G (the first step of "Back from the cookie", every skipped visit) lights 5; the press after it at the same step lights 6 alone; every other step plays 5 then 6 in phases (its cell blue with G computed, then green). A skip runs one pass per event: 2 3 (and 4) for a move, 5 for a skipped visit, 5 6 for a first visit. Short texts: the two updates of "Back from the cookie", without "the average of its returns" (said by line 6) and without "One step back".
- **The control figure's pseudocode** (titled "On-policy first-visit Monte Carlo control", in Spanish "Control Monte Carlo first-visit on-policy", the note's order): the same lines, with Q(s, a) and pairs, numbered 1 to 7: 1 Q(s, a) ← 0 and no returns, for every pair; π ε-greedy with respect to Q; 2 pick; 3 take; 4 G ← 0; 5 G ← γG + R_{t+1}; "If the pair S_t, A_t does not appear earlier in the episode:" (head); 6 Add G to its returns; Q(S_t, A_t) ← their average (update); 7 Make π ε-greedy in S_t with respect to Q (update: the cell's best move in bold). Moves 1 and 2 light 2 and 3 on their own presses ("A random first move" too); later moves of episode 1 play 2 then 3 (and 4 at the cookie) in phases as in the prediction figure. The skipped pair of "A move made twice" lights 5; every updating step plays 5, 6 and 7 in phases (G computed; the pair green, with the best move in bold already updated, since the engine draws it from Q; then the route band, on the slides that draw one). A skip's pass: 2 3 (4) for a move, 5 for a skipped pair, 5 6 7 for a first visit. Short texts: the choices of move 1 and of "A random first move" (without "The agent chooses its first move.", which line 2 says), move 2's choice ("the same table as its first") and the second step of "Back from the cookie", without "One step back".
