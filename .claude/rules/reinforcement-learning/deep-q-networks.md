## The rally, the note's game

- Screen 8 × 6, x = 0 the left column, y = 0 the top row; pixels 0 or 1 (the ball and the paddle lit); d = 48.
- The paddle: 2 pixels tall, in column 7, on rows 2–3 at every serve. Moves up, stay and down (a = 0, 1, 2) move it one pixel; its top row is clamped to 0–4, so a move into a wall leaves it in place.
- The ball: 1 pixel, one pixel diagonally per step in columns 0–6. It bounces off the top, bottom and left walls, showing for one screen on the wall's row or column: at row 0 it is rising, at row 5 falling, at column 0 moving left.
- Within a step the paddle moves, then the ball, then the check: when the ball arrives in column 6 moving right, it is hit if the paddle covers its row (r = +1, it turns back), or else missed (r = −1, terminal).
- A serve puts the ball at x = 0 on any of the 6 rows, moving right, rising or falling: 12 serves. Every serve can be played perfectly.
- After 10 hits a rally is cut, not ended: its last target still adds γ times the next screen's value. A perfect rally takes 114 steps (6 + 9 × 12). The prototype's 200-step limit never acts and is not mentioned.
- Exact values Q\* (γ = 0.9) by value iteration over the 552 true states (ball, direction, paddle): 1/(1 − 0.9^12) ≈ 1.394 one step before a hit, 0.9^11 × 1.39 ≈ 0.437 just after one, rising by 1/0.9 a step in between; 0.823 at every serve; for a miss certain k steps ahead, −0.9^(k−1): −1 in 18 states, −0.9 in 8, −0.81 in 2. Once every ball is hit, the 12 serves fall on two ball cycles of 60 steps, six serves each.
- Pairs of screens that can occur: 1,336 (6 at a serve, 1,330 after a move; 3,000,000 steps of random play meet exactly these). The plan's 1,560 counted each pair once per move that leads to it (up and stay give the same pair with the paddle at the top).
- Under random moves, episode 1 lasts 6 steps on 63% of seeds.

## The network and the run's settings

- The network: 96 inputs (the two screens' pixels), 32 rectifier units, 3 linear outputs; W₁ 32 × 97 and W₂ 3 × 33 with a first column of biases, 3,203 weights. Initialized uniformly within ±√(6/96) for the hidden layer and ±√(3/32) for the output, biases 0.
- γ = 0.9. ε = 1 − 0.9 (t − 1)/10,000 at step t, 0.1 from step 10,001. The memory keeps the last N = 10,000 transitions.
- Minibatches of 32 drawn uniformly with replacement, from the first step on, so the first minibatch is 32 copies of the one transition (the figures' choice; the paper does not say).
- Targets with θ as it stands before the step; no second network, no error clipping, no wait before learning.
- One plain gradient step per step on the mean of ½(y_j − Q(φ_j, a_j; θ))², α = 0.1; RMSProp is named as the paper's choice. k = 2 screens in φ; at a serve the first screen counts twice.
- 20,000 steps, 609 episodes for seed 48. Checks every 250 steps (81): greedy play from the 12 serves, the average largest value over 500 pairs of screens collected by random play before training, and the total reward per episode over 10,000 steps of ε = 0.05 play.
- Random streams: mulberry32 seeded with `subSeed(seed, stream)` (`lib/game.mjs`), one per use: 0 the weights, 1 the serves, 2 exploration, 3 the minibatch draws, 4 the fixed pairs of screens, 5 the evaluation; checks and evaluations never change a run. Figure 6's play uses mulberry32(777 + 48), which never touches training.
- Exact operations only (+, −, ×, ÷, comparisons, floor, min, max, `Math.sqrt`, `Math.imul`), so a rerun gives the same numbers bit for bit, and they match the prototype's (`understand/pongRally/dqn.mjs`).
- Why α = 0.1 (48 seeds, 20,000 steps): the first update passes its target on 1 of 48 seeds at α = 0.1 (median factor 0.64), 22 at 0.15, 44 at 0.2 and 48 at 0.3 (median 1.90, up to 3.2); the median first perfect check is 8,250, 6,000, 5,000 and 4,250; perfect at every check to step 20,000: 47 of 48 from a median of 11,750, then 48 from 9,000, 6,500 and 5,250.
- Why seed 48: a 6-step episode 1 whose six updates all move toward their targets without passing them (first factor 0.71), the first hit in episode 2, at step 12, and the first perfect check at 7,750, one relapse at 8,000, perfect at every check from 8,250. It learns early: seeds 1 to 16 play perfectly for good from steps 10,000 to 16,500, and seed 14 not by 20,000. Slides 5 to 7 and Figure 5 rely on its timing; another seed means rereading their texts.
