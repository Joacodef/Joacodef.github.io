# The figure modules of the Reinforcement Learning notes

How `gridworld.js` and `cartpole.js` implement the design in `notes-reinforcement-learning.md`. After changing either, compare every figure press by press with the live site or the mockup.

## gridworld.js

- It holds the two worlds, `cookie` and `cliff`, built by one function (each with its own cell names, rewards, cell size, and texts for choosing and taking a move, in one press too, `chooseTakeText`, and for a policy that picks at random, `randomPick` and `randomTake`), the grid, the sounds and the slide controls. Each figure's script computes its own run and slides and hands them to `slideshow()`, with the cookie grid as `world` when it is not the cliff: `monte-carlo.js`, `td-learning.js` (TD(0), batch TD(0) and Monte Carlo, and Sarsa), `q-learning.js`, `n-step-bootstrapping.js`, `eligibility-traces.js`, `model-based.js` (Q-planning, R-Max, Dyna-Q, and R-Max and Dyna-Q on the cliff with a wall), `prediction-with-approximation.js` and `control-with-approximation.js` (both on the cart-pole, drawn by `cartpole.js`).
- Values: on the cliff, at most one decimal on phones, while the cookie grid's larger cells keep two. The best value of a cell is in bold: in ink over the heatmap, on a blue highlight with the heatmap off.
- The best move's blue triangle lies along the two half-diagonals of the opposite triangle, out to a fifth of their length (`ARROW`), so that its point sits at the middle; it shows only when one move is worth more than the others and its value shows (when that value is in bold), and not in the cell where the agent or its faint copy stands, whose dot would cover it (on the cookie grid, the triangle's edges stuck out under the dot). Rejected before it: the two half-diagonals lit as lines, which read as a "greater than" sign between two numbers on the cliff, and an arrow on every move, whose shared half-diagonals cannot take two colors; and, after it, a small triangle for every move whose quarter the heatmap fills (most blended into their quarter, low values showed as pale notches, and on the cliff the faint coral ones vanished).
- Build options: `taken` fades the yellow of the move being taken; `route` draws the route band. `valuePath` gives the route a state-value estimate leads along (in each cell, the move with the largest reward plus γ times the next cell's value), and a value the band runs over sits on a chip of the band's color. Over the heatmap, a route's band is a translucent blue that lets the colors show through, its values without chips, and each value's halo takes the color under it.
- The agent, walls and falls are ink. On the cookie grid the goal is a drawn cookie with a bite out of it (dough and chips mixed from the color tokens, light dough and dark chips in both themes), with +1 beside it; the agent eats it, so it is gone while the agent stands on the goal (shrinking and fading after a press) and back for the next episode. The sides of the cells are a shade stronger than the diagonals inside them. A move with marks of two kinds (a pair updated toward a target that uses its own value) shows both, the later one inside the earlier.
- A figure that estimates state values (`perCell: true` in `slideshow()`, with `V` in place of `Q`) draws no diagonals and one value per cell, above the agent; its green and red marks outline the whole cell, and only the move just chosen is still a triangle, in yellow.
- The model-based figures: R-Max shades the moves it has not tried (`unknown` in a build, a list of [cell, action]) in gray, each showing the R_max it assumes for it, and the shade lifts at the value iteration press after the move is counted; Dyna-Q shows the value of every move its model holds, 0 included (`shown`, a Set of cell * 4 + action), so that blank still means untried, and outlines the move just stored in its model in blue. A step simulated by a model (a planning step played on its own) is played by a faint copy of the agent, a light dashed dot that appears in the state picked and glides where the model says the move leads (`ghost`, `ghostFrom` and `ghostBump` in a build), while the agent stays where it is. A wall that stands between two cells for a while is a thick ink line along their shared side (`walls`, a list of [cell, cell]).
- In the frames of a skip, a wall hit shows the agent pressed against the wall every other frame (`bumpPose`, from `shake`); every figure's frames do this.
- The heatmap mixes green into the background up to `--heat-max` and coral up to 0.4 of that (the token's values are in `site.css`). The reader's settings are kept in localStorage as `gw-heat` and `gw-sound`.
- `slideshow()` returns `show(slides, frame)`, which starts a run from its first slide (the mode buttons of a figure with two runs call it). A build or frame can replace the status line with `label` (the batch figure's "Pass 147", the Q-planning figure's "Planning step 9", or "Episode 11, before the first move"). A build's `fit` sets a long readout equation smaller on phones (`.eq.fit`), such as an update with its rewards written out.
- A figure with its own drawing passes it to `slideshow()` as `view`; `noHeat` removes the heatmap from its settings.

## cartpole.js

- The classic cart-pole: cart 1 kg, pole 0.1 kg and 1 m, pushes of 10 N, Euler steps of 0.02 s, failure past 12° or ±2.4 m; +1 per step, γ = 1. In `stepCP`'s Euler step the next angle is θ + τθ̇ with the old θ̇, so the next state's range of the angle does not depend on the push. It also has the episodes under the random policy, and the drawings.
- `createCartPole(svg)`, the prediction note's view: the cart and pole above the bars, a dot on the angle axis at the current angle, the push just chosen as a yellow arrow beside the cart, green and red outlines on whole ranges, and `noHeat`. `angleChart` draws one bar per range of the angle, on an axis of the steps until the pole falls, with a small cart and pole under each range at its middle angle.
- The control note's drawings: `pushChart` draws the 24 weights in two panels, swinging left above and swinging right below, two bars per range (push left, push right, named by small arrows under them), a small cart and pole under each range with a curved arrow for its swing (`swingArc`) and a small blue arrow for the push worth more (none on a tie); the angles are labeled once, under the lower panel; an axis that goes below 0 grows bars down from a line at 0. `createPushView` adds the cart and pole on their track (`cartStage`, which `createCartPole` shares), a curved arrow at the pole's tip, and a dot on the angle axis of the panel of the swing, kept within ±12°. Its marks: the pair just chosen filled yellow (`pick`), the pair updated outlined green, the pair a target borrows red, an n-step window dashed blue (`win`), and a frame around a situation (`sel`). `featNo` numbers the weights w1 to w24, panel by panel, swinging left first.
