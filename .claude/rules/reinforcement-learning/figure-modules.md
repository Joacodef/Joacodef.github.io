# The figure modules of the Reinforcement Learning notes

How `gridworld.js`, `cartpole.js` and `pong.js` implement the design in `notes-reinforcement-learning.md`. After changing any of them, compare every figure that uses it press by press with the live site or the mockup.

## gridworld.js

- It holds the two worlds, `cookie` and `cliff`, built by one function (each with its own cell names, rewards, cell size, and texts for choosing and taking a move, in one press too, `chooseTakeText`, and for a policy that picks at random, `randomPick` and `randomTake`), the grid, the sounds and the slide controls. Each figure's script computes its own run and slides and hands them to `slideshow()`, with the cookie grid as `world` when it is not the cliff: `monte-carlo.js`, `td-learning.js` (TD(0), batch TD(0) and Monte Carlo, and Sarsa), `q-learning.js`, `n-step-bootstrapping.js`, `eligibility-traces.js`, `model-based.js` (Q-planning, R-Max, Dyna-Q, and R-Max and Dyna-Q on the cliff with a wall), `prediction-with-approximation.js` and `control-with-approximation.js` (both on the cart-pole, drawn by `cartpole.js`).
- Values: on the cliff, at most one decimal on phones, while the cookie grid's larger cells keep two. The best value of a cell is in bold: in ink over the heatmap, on a blue highlight with the heatmap off.
- The best move's blue triangle lies along the two half-diagonals of the opposite triangle, out to a fifth of their length (`ARROW`), so that its point sits at the middle; it shows only when one move is worth more than the others and its value shows (when that value is in bold), and not in the cell where the agent or its faint copy stands, whose dot would cover it (on the cookie grid, the triangle's edges stuck out under the dot). Rejected before it: the two half-diagonals lit as lines, which read as a "greater than" sign between two numbers on the cliff, and an arrow on every move, whose shared half-diagonals cannot take two colors; and, after it, a small triangle for every move whose quarter the heatmap fills (most blended into their quarter, low values showed as pale notches, and on the cliff the faint coral ones vanished).
- Build options: `taken` fades the yellow of the move being taken; `route` draws the route band. `valuePath` gives the route a state-value estimate leads along (in each cell, the move with the largest reward plus γ times the next cell's value), and a value the band runs over sits on a chip of the band's color. Over the heatmap, a route's band is a translucent blue that lets the colors show through, its values without chips, and each value's halo takes the color under it.
- The agent, walls and falls are ink. On the cookie grid the goal is a drawn cookie with a bite out of it (dough and chips mixed from the color tokens, light dough and dark chips in both themes), with +1 beside it; the agent eats it, so it is gone while the agent stands on the goal (shrinking and fading after a press) and back for the next episode. The sides of the cells are a shade stronger than the diagonals inside them. A move with marks of two kinds (a pair updated toward a target that uses its own value) shows both, the later one inside the earlier.
- A figure that estimates state values (`perCell: true` in `slideshow()`, with `V` in place of `Q`) draws no diagonals and one value per cell, above the agent; its green and red marks outline the whole cell, and only the move just chosen is still a triangle, in yellow.
- The model-based figures: R-Max shades the moves it has not tried (`unknown` in a build, a list of [cell, action]) in gray, each showing the R_max it assumes for it, and the shade lifts at the value iteration press after the move is counted; Dyna-Q shows the value of every move its model holds, 0 included (`shown`, a Set of cell * 4 + action), so that blank still means untried, and outlines the move just stored in its model in blue. A step simulated by a model (a planning step played on its own) is played by a faint copy of the agent, a light dashed dot that appears in the state picked and glides where the model says the move leads (`ghost`, `ghostFrom` and `ghostBump` in a build), while the agent stays where it is. A wall that stands between two cells for a while is a thick ink line along their shared side (`walls`, a list of [cell, cell]).
- In the frames of a skip, a wall hit shows the agent pressed against the wall every other frame (`bumpPose`, from `shake`); every figure's frames do this.
- The heatmap mixes green into the background up to `--heat-max` and coral up to 0.4 of that (the token's values are in `site.css`). The reader's settings are kept in localStorage as `gw-heat` and `gw-sound`.
- `slideshow()` returns `show(slides, frame)`, which starts a run from its first slide (the mode buttons of a figure with two runs call it). A build or frame can replace the status line with `label` (the batch figure's "Pass 147", the Q-planning figure's "Planning step 9", or "Episode 11, before the first move"). A build's `fit` sets a long readout equation smaller on phones (`.eq.fit`), such as an update with its rewards written out.
- A figure with its own drawing passes it to `slideshow()` as `view`; `noHeat` removes the heatmap from its settings.
- The grid's shapes: `triPts(c)` gives a cell's four triangles, `trapPts(c)` the same without their inner tips (out to `ARROW` of the half-diagonals; whole triangles with `perCell`), used by every fill and mark, and `tipPts(c, a)` the tip of triangle a, drawn once per cell as `.gw-tip`; `shrink` insets any polygon toward the mean of its corners.
- Pseudocode: `slideshow({ code: { title, lines: [{ html, kind, head, ind }] } })` puts a `.pcode` panel before the readout (`.fig.steps.with-code`). `kind` is pick (yellow), take or plain (gray), model (blue), update (green) or target (red: a line that computes a return, target or TD error), the line's color when lit, `head` a loop's header (never lit, not numbered), `ind` its depth. A build lists the line numbers it plays in `code` (its last one stays lit, or all in turn when it animates, 150 ms each, faster for long lists), and may give `phases`, `[{ lines, draw, slow }]`, which `playPhases` draws on the grid as each phase's first line lights, `slow` phases at 150 ms a line with animation, the rest sharing what is left of 2 s, and then the build itself; a frame's `code` is one pass for a skip. With a panel, the readout shows only the current press, in its `short` text when the build has one. No fade on the lines: they are lit for as little as 45 ms. `show(slides, frame, code)` swaps the pseudocode with the run (the mode figures), and `.fig.steps.with-code:has(> .modes)` puts the buttons above the drawing. In a skip, each pass of the loop keeps the lines of the frame it began on, so that an episode ending partway through a pass does not mix two. A drawing may give `bestQ`, the table the bold best value and the blue arrow come from, so that a phase can show a new value before the line that improves the policy (the Monte Carlo control figure).

## cartpole.js

- The classic cart-pole: cart 1 kg, pole 0.1 kg and 1 m, pushes of 10 N, Euler steps of 0.02 s, failure past 12° or ±2.4 m; +1 per step, γ = 1. In `stepCP`'s Euler step the next angle is θ + τθ̇ with the old θ̇, so the next state's range of the angle does not depend on the push. It also has the episodes under the random policy, and the drawings.
- `createCartPole(svg)`, the prediction note's view: the cart and pole above the bars, a dot on the angle axis at the current angle, the push just chosen as a yellow arrow beside the cart, green and red outlines on whole ranges, and `noHeat`. `angleChart` draws one bar per range of the angle, on an axis of the steps until the pole falls, with a small cart and pole under each range at its middle angle.
- The control note's drawings: `pushChart` draws the 24 weights in two panels, swinging left above and swinging right below, two bars per range (push left, push right, named by small arrows under them), a small cart and pole under each range with a curved arrow for its swing (`swingArc`) and a small blue arrow for the push worth more (none on a tie); the angles are labeled once, under the lower panel; an axis that goes below 0 grows bars down from a line at 0. `createPushView` adds the cart and pole on their track (`cartStage`, which `createCartPole` shares), a curved arrow at the pole's tip, and a dot on the angle axis of the panel of the swing, kept within ±12°. Its marks: the pair just chosen filled yellow (`pick`), the pair updated outlined green, the pair a target borrows red, an n-step window dashed blue (`win`), and a frame around a situation (`sel`). `featNo` numbers the weights w1 to w24, panel by panel, swinging left first.

## pong.js

- The DQN note's game and its drawings, shared by its Figures 1, 2 and 3: a screen of 8 × 6 pixels (x = 0 the left column, y = 0 the top row), the ball one pixel in columns 0 to 6, the paddle two pixels in column 7, the moves 0 up, 1 stay and 2 down (`MOVES`, through `tr`). The comment at the top of the module is the contract with the figure script, which the figure code follows: a state `{ ball, prev, paddle, prevPaddle }` (`prev` null at a serve, `prevPaddle` optional, the paddle on the earlier screen) and the fields of a build (`s`, `q`, `exact`, `pick`, `taken`, `tg`, `upd`, `best`, `oneScreen`, `nudge`, `memory`, `learn`, `jump`). The figures hand it recorded values: it computes nothing but the drawing.
- `createScreen(parent, { x, y, scale })` draws a state, φ's two screens in one: each pixel a rect filled through `shade()` (`plane.js`), the later screen at 255, the earlier screen's ball, and the paddle's pixels where it moved, at `FAINT` = 100, unlit pixels at 0; faint lines between the pixels (`.pg-grid`, `--img-hi` at 14% into `--img-lo`), the frame (`.px-frame`), and the top, bottom and left walls in ink-2 just outside it (`.pg-wall`, 3 units wide at 24 units per pixel, 2 on the 12-unit screens), the right edge, behind the paddle, left thin. In the light theme the screen is a near-black block; in the dark one its unlit pixels are the card, and the frame and walls show where it is. The paddle is drawn over its column, so that a skip's frame (`nudge`, −1 or 1) can push it into a wall. Marks on the screen are in `--img-hi`, never ink, which is an unlit pixel in the light theme: One frame mode's dashed earlier ball (`.pg-unseen`, `oneScreen` in a build) and the focus ring of a handle on the screen (`.handle.on-screen`). After Next, the pixels that light switch at once and those that dim fade over 260 ms. It returns what a drag needs: `cx`, `cy` (the middle of a pixel) and `toPixel`. Figure 2 draws its two small screens with it.
- `createGame(svg, { decimals })` is region A (Figures 1 and 3, y 0 to 178 of a 440-unit-wide viewBox): the screen at 24 units per pixel (x 12–204, y 18–162), the best move's small blue arrow at x 208–222 beside the paddle's middle (a triangle up or down, a short dash for stay, `.pg-best`; none on a tie, before the first screen or after the end), and `valueBars`. Each move is a row: its name at x 236 and its value right-aligned at x 436 on one line, and its bar below them. The axis runs from −2 to 2.5 over x 236–434 (zero at 324, 44 units to a unit of value), numbered at −2 to 2, with short faint lines at each tick within each row and a solid one at 0; under it the two ways, "← a miss ahead" at its left end and "hits ahead →" at its right end, each on a line of its own, then its name, "value of the move, Q(φ, a; θ)". The plan's layout, the names right-aligned at x 258 beside bars on x 264–396, did not fit: at the phones' 17 units "quedarse" is 69 units wide and would have run over the arrow and the screen, and a six-character value over the bars' end; and on one line the two ways read as "a miss ahead hits ahead" on phones, while the Spanish ones do not fit one.
- The bars are the cart-pole's (`.cp-bar`, `.cp-bar-top`): they grow from 0 and glide over 260 ms, and a value outside the axis stops at its end with a zigzag notch. Values have `decimals` decimals (2 in Figure 1, 3 in Figure 3), the largest in bold (`.pg-val.best`). The tie rule, `bestMoves`: every move whose value equals the largest at the shown precision is the best, so a tie shows two values in bold and no arrow. The marks are `pushChart`'s, on a row's bar: the move just chosen filled yellow (`pick`, `.gw-pick`; `taken` fades it over 300 ms), the bars updated and borrowed by a target outlined in green and red (`upd`, `tg`, `.gw-mark`), a second outline 2 units inside the first; `exact` draws short red lines across the rows (`.cp-ref`). With `q` null the bars give way to "end"; with `q` left out the rows show without bars.
- Region M, the replay memory (y 182–266): the label "Replay memory 𝒟: 6 of 10,000" (𝒟 in the serif, the numbers through `localNum`), then either 12 cells of 32 × 30 units, each stored transition with its move's glyph (`moveGlyph`) and its reward when not 0, numbered under it, the empty ones dashed, and the cells drawn outlined in green with "×n" above them (`.pg-count`); or a strip of the transitions stored, oldest at the left, in faint ink (`.pg-strip`), with a green tick above each slot drawn (`.pg-draw`), draws within 2.5 units of each other sharing one tick 7 + 4(n − 1) units tall (22 at most), and its ends labeled "oldest" and "newest".
- Region B, learning from one transition (y 276–410, hidden when `learn` is null and in a skip's frames): the label "Learning from transition 12", the two pairs of screens at 12 units per pixel (x 12 and 214) named φ_j and φ_j+1 (φ_1 and φ_2 with `sub: 1`), "end" in place of the second when it is terminal, the arrow between them labeled with the move and the reward ("stay, 0"), and the gauge on x 324–432, from the smaller of the value and the target to the larger: the value's tick and number in blue, the target's in red, each named, the labels hanging inward from the ends, and a green-outlined marker (`.pg-marker`) that slides from the value to the new one (`next`) over 400 ms.
- `createPongView(svg)` is Figure 3's view for `slideshow()`: a 440 × 410 viewBox with regions A, M and B stacked between faint rules at y 180 and 271, so that nothing moves as the readout grows; it returns `{ draw, setHeat() {}, noHeat: true }`, so the settings offer the sound alone. With reduced motion nothing animates.
- Its classes in `notes.css` start with `pg-`: labels and values at 13 units, axis names at 12 (17 and 15 on phones, under `max-width: 560px`, where the figure is drawn at 0.71 to 0.55 of its size), "end" at 16 (20), the φ names in the serif at 15 (19). Checked on a test page at 1280, 390 and 320 px, in both languages and both themes (`prefers-color-scheme` and `data-theme`): every label inside its viewBox and clear of the others and of the marks, the faint ball at a contrast of 3.2 (dark) to 3.5 (light) against an unlit pixel, the dashed ball and the focus ring at 8 or more, and nothing wider than the page.
