# Reinforcement Learning notes

What holds only for the notes of Prof. Rodrigo Toro's Reinforcement Learning course (IIC3675) at UC Chile, on top of `notes.md`.

## Sources

- The course README, `course-materials/reinforcement-learning/README.md`, has the syllabus and note status (with the sections still missing a figure), the notation and glossary tables, and how to read the PDFs.
- The course follows Sutton and Barto, *Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction* (2nd ed., 2020), and its readings are the book's chapters. They are saved in `course-materials/reinforcement-learning/`, grouped by exam rather than by chapter. The book is the reference for theory and formula notation. Its PDFs lose Greek letters and formulas in `pdftotext`, so render the pages with formulas to read them (the course README says how).
- The first exam's study guide and the class slides (in Spanish) give the sections each exam covers and the course's Spanish terms. When the slides cover more of a chapter than its PDF, as class 11 does for chapter 12 (up to tabular Sarsa(λ)), the note follows the slides' scope with the book's formulas.
- Beyond the book: the class 12 slides teach R-Max, which the book does not cover. For R-Max the slides are the reference, with their notation: the counts N(s, a) and N(s, a, s′, r) (the pseudocode writes N_t and N_p), the estimate P(s′, r | s, a) = N(s, a, s′, r)/N(s, a) (capital P, against the book's p for the true dynamics), the number of visits k and the reward R_max. A pair is known once N(s, a) ≥ k; the slides' pseudocode writes > k, and the notes follow ≥ k. An unknown pair gives R_max and ends the episode, and value iteration runs on that model at every step. The original is Brafman and Tennenholtz, "R-max: a general polynomial time algorithm for near-optimal reinforcement learning" (JMLR 2002), which the model-based note names in its text.
- Never reuse the book's examples or the study guide's exercises: not the cliff, the windy gridworld, the random walk, driving home, blackjack, Jack's car rental, the gambler, tic-tac-toe, the maximization bias MDP with states A and B, the 10-armed testbed, or chapter 8's sum of a dozen dice, Dyna maze, blocking maze and shortcut maze, and not the exercises of the study guide (the sushi cart, the cave) or of the slides (in class 12, the grid where a key opens a door past a field of spikes, its table of rewards per 1,000 steps, the table of episode lengths on a maze, and the maze before and after an earthquake). Design each note's own worlds and numbers, and compute every number the page quotes with a script.
- Exception, decided by Joaquín on 29 Sep 2026: the interactive figures use the professor's 3 × 3 cookie grid and the cliff, the worlds he uses across several classes, so the figures look like what the class shows. The notes' text keeps its own examples, except where the cliff is introduced and compared: section 5 of the TD note, where the reader first meets it with the Sarsa figure, and the Q-learning note, whose text also uses it (decided the same day): its own example had been a smaller cliff, and one world lets the text and the figure support each other.

## Interactive figures

- A figure is a guided run that the reader steps through like slides, with Next and Back, and each press is one line of the method's pseudocode as the note writes it: choose A (the chosen move turns yellow before the agent moves), take it, choose A′ when the method does, build the target or TD error, update. Each choice is explained once, where it happens: Sarsa-type methods choose A′ before updating, from the table as it stands, and Q-learning chooses after. The Monte Carlo figures play the episode with the table unchanged, the moves made (or, for state values, the cells left) outlined in dashed blue, then sweep back one step per press (G ← γG + R, then the average), turning each update green. Every step that updates shows that update in its readout, the value in green taking G, computed in red, or the average of its returns; a skipped visit shows only G (asked by Joaquín on 2 Oct 2026). TD(0)'s figure predicts values for a fixed random policy: choose (the policy's pick, in yellow), take, the TD error with its target, update; after move 1, choosing and taking share a press. Every step of the first episode is played, never skipped, in every figure (asked by Joaquín on 1 Oct 2026): the first moves go through every line, and the rest of episode 1 gets at least one press per move, combining lines (choosing and taking in one press, or a move with its TD error). A run's first episode must therefore be short, which decides its seed; on the cliff that is rare (one-step Sarsa's first episode has a median of 145 moves, and 413 of 60,000 seeds give 20 or fewer). On the cookie grid, whose episodes are short, a slide in episode 2 is also reached by playing that episode move by move (and its sweep step by step), not by a skip of a few moves into its middle, which looks odd (Joaquín, the same day). From episode 2 on, slides skip to the moments where something new is learned, playing the skipped moves quickly and saying how many were skipped, and may combine lines. The last slide adds Reset. Each new figure still starts with a mockup. A small figure about a single decision can instead be something the reader drags, as in the computer vision notes: the model-based note's corridor figure, in section 2, moves the untried door.
- The grid: each cell the agent can stand on is split by its diagonals into four triangles, one per move, each showing that move's value (blank while untried; on the cliff, at most one decimal on phones, while the cookie grid's larger cells keep two), with the best value of the cell in bold: in ink over the heatmap, on a blue highlight with the heatmap off. Marks on the triangles: yellow (`--pick`) for the move just chosen, which fades while the agent takes it (`taken` in a build; asked by Joaquín on 2 Oct 2026), green (`--ok`) for moves being updated (as strong as their trace, in Sarsa(λ)), red for the move or moves a target uses, and dashed blue for the steps of an n-step window. There is no cell selection, and no arrow on the move being made: the agent's movement shows each move. The one arrow is the best move's: over the heatmap, a small blue triangle at the middle of a cell points toward it (Joaquín's idea, 5 Oct 2026, because a strongly filled triangle points toward the middle, against its own move, and looked like an arrow the wrong way). It lies along the two half-diagonals of the opposite triangle, out to a fifth of their length (`ARROW` in `gridworld.js`), so that its point sits at the middle; it shows only when one move is worth more than the others and its value shows (when that value is in bold), and not in the cell where the agent or its faint copy stands, whose dot would cover it (on the cookie grid, the triangle's edges stuck out under the dot). Tried before it: lighting the two half-diagonals as lines, which read as a "greater than" sign between two numbers on the cliff, and an arrow on every move, whose shared half-diagonals cannot take two colors. A slide about the route the best moves trace (a first way through, the shortest path, the end of the run) draws that route as a light blue band from the start to the goal, under the values, ending in an arrowhead, and the values it runs over sit on the band's color (`route` in a build; asked for by Joaquín on 1 Oct 2026, after a thin line struck through the numbers). Every figure ends with such a band: the cookie grid figures that estimate state values draw the route their estimates lead along (`valuePath`: in each cell, the move with the largest reward plus γ times the next cell's value), and a value the band runs over sits on a chip of the band's color (asked by Joaquín on 2 Oct 2026). The agent, walls and falls are ink. On the cookie grid the goal is a drawn cookie with a bite out of it (dough and chips mixed from the color tokens, light dough and dark chips in both themes), with +1 beside it; the agent eats it, so it is gone while the agent stands on the goal (shrinking and fading after a press) and back for the next episode. The sides of the cells are a shade stronger than the diagonals inside them. A move with marks of two kinds (a pair updated toward a target that uses its own value) shows both, the later one inside the earlier. A figure that estimates state values (`perCell: true` in `slideshow()`, with `V` in place of `Q`) draws no diagonals and one value per cell, above the agent; its green and red marks outline the whole cell, and only the move just chosen is still a triangle, in yellow. The model-based figures add two things (5 Oct 2026): R-Max shades the moves it has not tried (`unknown` in a build, a list of [cell, action]) in gray, each showing the R_max it assumes for it, and the shade lifts at the value iteration press after the move is counted; Dyna-Q shows the value of every move its model holds, 0 included (`shown`, a Set of cell * 4 + action), so that blank still means untried, and outlines the move just stored in its model in blue. A step simulated by a model (a planning step played on its own) is played by a faint copy of the agent, a light dashed dot that appears in the state picked and glides where the model says the move leads (`ghost`, `ghostFrom` and `ghostBump` in a build), while the agent stays where it is; the Q-planning figure plays every step with it, and Dyna-Q's planning steps that have a slide of their own. A wall that stands between two cells for a while is a thick ink line along their shared side (`walls`, a list of [cell, cell]).
- The heatmap (decided with Joaquín on 5 Oct 2026, after testing several scales): under the lines, each value shown fills its triangle (by move, the default) or each cell takes its best value (by cell; a figure with one value per cell fills the cell either way), green (`--ok`) as a positive value nears 1, by its square so that 0.73, 0.81, 0.9 and 1 stay apart, and coral (`--coral`, a red toward orange mixed from `--red` and `--pick`) as a negative one nears −10, the deepest from there on; 0 is not shaded, so a fresh grid stays clean. Green is mixed into the background up to `--heat-max` (60% light, 45% dark), and coral up to 0.4 of that, so that costs recede: a strong color reads as the move to pick, and Joaquín found that the cliff's purple, strongest at the falls and the far corner, looked like a preferred choice (5 Oct 2026). A scale that turned the cliff's values greener as they neared 0 would not work either: in each cliff figure's first episode, the moves tried once into a wall (−0.5, −0.75) would be the greenest on the grid while the agent picks among the blank untried ones, worth 0. R-Max's untried moves keep their gray shade and take no color until known. Over the heatmap, the best value of a cell is in bold ink instead of on its blue highlight, with the blue triangle that points to its move, a route's band is a translucent blue that lets the colors show through, its values without chips, and each value's halo takes the color under it. With the heatmap off, the figures are drawn exactly as before it existed.
- Settings: each figure has a round button with a gear at the end of its controls, after Next (Reset appears before it), that opens a menu above it for that figure only: the heatmap (by move, by cell, off) and the sound (on, off). A reader's choices are kept (localStorage `gw-heat` and `gw-sound`) as the start for the next figures and pages; Escape closes the menu, and the arrow keys inside it do not change slides. Joaquín asked for settings that belong to the figure they change, not a bar for the whole page, and the button sits in the controls rather than on the grid, where it would cover values and the cookie's +1.
- A figure with two runs, such as the wall figure's R-Max and Dyna-Q, picks one with `.modes` buttons above the drawing (`slideshow()` returns `show(slides, frame)`, which starts that run from its first slide). A build or frame can replace the status line with `label` (the Q-planning figure's "Planning step 9", or "Episode 11, before the first move").
- The grid, the marks and the presses are explained once, in the lead-in of the first figure a reader meets: the Monte Carlo prediction figure in section 1 of the Monte Carlo note, on the cookie grid, with one value per cell; its lead-in also introduces the heatmap's green and the settings button. The control figure in section 4 adds the values of moves, in triangles, with the best one in bold and the blue triangle that points to it, and the heatmap by cell. The TD(0) figure in section 1 of the TD note learns from the same 30 episodes as the prediction figure (the same seed) and introduces the red mark, the Sarsa figure in section 5 introduces the cliff and the heatmap's coral for negative values, and the later figures' lead-ins and first slides say only what is new (Q-learning's choice after the update and its maximum, the n-step window, the traces; in the model-based note, the faint copy in section 1, R-Max's gray shade in section 2, the model's empty start in section 3) and link back to it.
- A slide claims only what holds in its run at every move, not only at the slides shown. One-step Sarsa with α = 0.5 does not settle on a route within 30 episodes (8 of 60,000 seeds do by episode 22), since its target uses the random next move and the open cells' values stay within a few tenths of each other; its figure builds on what does hold, a fall picked as A′ pushing Q(A2, right) below Q(A2, up) for good.
- The controls sit right under the grid, with Back and Next first, so that they never move as the text grows: readers press Next quickly. New text appears only below the old, which dims. While the grid animates, Back and Next are frozen, so that quick presses cannot pile one animation on another (asked by Joaquín on 5 Oct 2026): during a skip they look disabled (`aria-disabled`, which keeps the focus on them, so that the arrow keys work again when it ends), and after a press they ignore presses for about half a second, until the move has played out; with reduced motion nothing animates, and nothing is frozen.
- The figures share `src/assets/js/notes/reinforcement-learning/gridworld.js`: the two worlds, `cookie` and `cliff`, built by one function (each with its own cell names, rewards, cell size, and texts for choosing and taking a move, in one press too, `chooseTakeText`, and for a policy that picks at random, `randomPick` and `randomTake`), the grid, the sounds and the slide controls. Each figure's script computes its own run and slides and hands them to `slideshow()`, with the cookie grid as `world` when it is not the cliff: `monte-carlo.js`, `td-learning.js` (TD(0) and Sarsa), `q-learning.js`, `n-step-bootstrapping.js`, `eligibility-traces.js` and `model-based.js` (Q-planning, R-Max, Dyna-Q, and R-Max and Dyna-Q on the cliff with a wall). Q-planning's presses: pick a state and a move, the model's answer, the update, then one press per planning step. R-Max's presses follow the slides' loop: choose, take, count, then value iteration on the new model, which is also the first line of the next move, outlining every value it changes in green; Dyna-Q's are choose, take, the Q-learning update, the model, and planning, whose steps after the cookie get a slide each. In the frames of a skip, a wall hit shows the agent pressed against the wall every other frame (`bumpPose`, from `shake` in `gridworld.js`), so that a run of hits shakes it there; every figure's frames do this, and a skip over a run of hits says so in its note (the wall figure's "The agent hits the wall again and again, and stays in A2"). A figure that claims something about the rest of its run checks it in code and warns in the console when it fails. A build's `fit` sets a long readout equation smaller on phones (`.eq.fit`), such as an update with its rewards written out; check every figure at 320 px for equations wider than their box. After changing the module, compare each figure press by press with the live site or the mockup, drawing and text, to check that nothing else moved.
- Sounds made in the browser (a click for a move, a duller one for a wall, a falling tone for a fall, a soft crunch when the agent eats the cookie, a chime at the end) play only after Next or the right arrow key, and the figure's settings turn them off.
- Figures must stay light on any reader's device. The run behind a figure is small and computed once, from a fixed seed, so every reader sees the same run: the cliff's 30 episodes are about 650 to 900 moves and take a few milliseconds when the page loads. Animations play only after a press, last at most about two seconds and stop by themselves; with reduced motion they are replaced by an immediate change. Nothing runs in the background.

## Notation

- The book's symbols, as the course README's notation table lists them: `S_t`, `A_t`, `R_{t+1}`, `G_t`, `γ`, `π`, `b`, `v_π`, `q_π`, `q_*`, `V`, `Q`, `α`, `ε`, `G_{t:t+n}`, `ρ_{t:h}`, `V̄`, for chapter 12 `λ`, `G_t^λ` and `z_t`, and for chapter 8 `p(s′, r | s, a)` (the true dynamics), `Model(S, A)` and `n` (planning steps). R-Max uses the slides' `N(s, a)`, `N(s, a, s′, r)`, `P(s′, r | s, a)`, `k` and `R_max` (written `<i>R</i><sub>max</sub>`); the value of a move under R-Max's model is written `Q(s, a)`, and value iteration's update uses `V`.
- In HTML, write time indices as subscripts with italic letters (`<i>S</i><sub><i>t</i>+1</sub>`), the optimal values as `<i>q</i><sub>*</sub>`, the update arrow as `←`, and a maximum as `max<sub><i>a</i></sub>`. Write state and action names upright: `<i>Q</i>(start, bridge)`. Stack a superscript over a subscript with `.ss` (`<i>G</i><span class="ss"><span>λ</span><span><i>t</i></span></span>` for G_t^λ), the limits of a sum or product with `.lims`, an expected value's bar with `.ovl` (V̄), and the book's bold vectors with `<b class="vec">w</b>`. The eligibility trace is z, as in the book's 2nd edition; the notes mention that it is also written e, as in the slides.
- In code snippets, name variables after the symbols: `q`, `q1`, `q2`, `alpha`, `gamma`, `eps`, `pi`, `g` (a return or a target), `rho`, `z`, `lam` (λ; `lambda` is a Python keyword), `s_next`, `a_next`.
- Spanish terms come from the study guide and the slides, which keep many terms in English: greedy, ε-greedy, bandit, Sarsa, Q-values, value iteration, first-visit, every-visit, baseline, n-step TD, n-step Sarsa, 4-step Sarsa, TD error, eligibility traces, λ-return. The Spanish notes keep those in English too, as well as Q-learning, Expected Sarsa, Double Q-learning, on-policy, off-policy, bootstrapping, afterstates, forward view and backward view (glossed as vista hacia adelante and hacia atrás), and, from class 12, model-based RL, model-free, planning (with the verb planear), Q-planning, R-Max, Dyna, Dyna-Q, near-optimal, and known and unknown, in italics. The rest follows the glossary in the course README: retorno, retorno de n pasos, recompensa, política, política de comportamiento, política objetivo, objetivo (of an update), estado terminal, episodio, tamaño de paso α, descuento γ, sesgo de maximización, razón de muestreo por importancia, modelo, modelo de distribución, modelo de muestra, experiencia simulada, aprendizaje del modelo (model learning), RL directo (direct RL), control de búsqueda (search control), barrido (of value iteration), optimistas ante lo desconocido. A note is an apunte (el apunte de TD), not a nota.

## Ink

- Blue ink (`.ln`, `.ln-path`): routes, the paths an agent takes through a world, and the transitions they are made of, including those a model holds, and in the grid figures the triangle that points to a cell's best move, the move a greedy route takes. In a diagram that sets a model against the world (section 4 of the model-based note), blue is what the model says and ink the world: a door the world has locked is an ink wall (`.wall-line`), one only the model believes locked a blue wall (`.wall-line.model`), and a way the world has open that the model does not know dashed ink (`.ink-dash`). Where an untried move leads is a dashed blue outline (`.state.unk`).
- Carmine (`.pt`, `.pt-dot`): the target of an update, the value an estimate moves toward, and the TD error that carries it back. In formulas, the target is wrapped in `.pt`, in the Monte Carlo note too, where it is the return G (its legend says so, and its figures' readouts show G red where it is computed and averaged in; asked by Joaquín on 2 Oct 2026); in backup diagrams, the nodes the target is built from are carmine; in timelines, the estimate a target bootstraps from.
- In the grid figures, yellow (`--pick`) marks the move an agent has just chosen, and green (`--ok`) a value being updated. Under the values, the heatmap shades positive values green (`--ok`) and negative ones a faint coral (`--coral`), so green is both the fill of a high value and the outline of a value being updated, the outline drawn over the fill (decided with Joaquín on 5 Oct 2026). Coral is a red toward orange, and pale, so that a cost's fill stays apart from the strong carmine outline of a target. In a figure's readout, the value being updated, the left side of the update's arrow, is green (`.upd`), in the presses whose grid outlines it in green, as the target is red like its red outline (asked by Joaquín on 2 Oct 2026).
- Everything else, including states, the pair being updated and the edges of backup diagrams, is drawn in ink.
- Backup diagrams follow the book: an open circle is a state, a small filled dot an action, and an arc across branches a maximum. The pair being updated is at the top.

## The notes' own examples

- **Monte Carlo and TD: the wandering home.** Kitchen, hallway, study and charger (terminal), γ = 1, −1 per move. The robot's random policy: kitchen → hallway always; hallway → study or kitchen, ½ each; study → charger or hallway, ½ each. Its values are −9, −8 and −5; q_π(study, charger) = −1, q_π(study, hallway) = −9, q_π(hallway, study) = −6, q_π(hallway, kitchen) = −10. Two trips, kitchen-hallway-study and kitchen-hallway-kitchen-hallway-study-hallway-study, give first-visit estimates −5, −4, −2 and every-visit −5, −3.5, −1.67; after 10,000 simulated trips both are within 0.1 of the true values. The greedy policy is optimal (−3, −2, −1); ε-greedy around it with ε = 0.2 has values −3.47, −2.47, −1.25; in the study, Σπ′q_π = 0.9(−1) + 0.1(−9) = −1.8 ≥ −5. TD(0) with α = 0.5 from −5, −4, −2 on the trip kitchen-hallway-kitchen-hallway-study-charger: TD errors 0, −2, −1, 2, 1, table −5.5, −4, −1.5 (constant-α Monte Carlo: −4, −3, −1.5); with the table fixed the TD errors are 0, −2, 0, 1, 1 and add up to G_0 − V(kitchen) = 0. Batch example: hallway −1 → study once (charger taken, 0), study +1 three times and 0 once: V(study) = 0.6 for both, V(hallway) = −1 for batch Monte Carlo and −0.4 for batch TD(0). Sarsa escaping the shuttle: Q(hallway, kitchen) = 0.5(−1 + 0 − 0) = −0.5.
- **The cookie grid.** The professor's grid, as in the class 8 slides: 3 × 3, start A1 (bottom left), cookie C3 (top right, +1, ends the episode), 0 for every other move, γ = 0.9; under the random policy its values are 0.31 0.51 0 / 0.25 0.34 0.51 / 0.20 0.25 0.31, top row first.
- **Monte Carlo, section 1: the prediction figure.** First-visit Monte Carlo prediction for the random policy (each move ¼), every V at 0, seed 15395 (the TD(0) figure's, so the two learn from the same episodes), 30 episodes, 702 moves. Episode 1: 5 moves, A1 up, A2 left into the wall, A2 right, B2 right, C2 up; its sweep gives C2 1, B2 0.9, skips A2's 0.81 at t = 2 and gives A2 0.73 from its first visit, and A1 0.66. Episode 3 takes 41 moves: A1's return is about 0.015, and its average falls from 0.59 to 0.40. After 30 episodes the averages are 0.37 0.55 · / 0.34 0.45 0.62 / 0.27 0.26 0.44, all above v_π and at most 0.13 from it, in C1, which has the fewest returns (15; A1 has 30); they swap the order of A1 and B1. The route the estimates lead along is A1 A2 B2 C2 C3, the shortest. Batch updating on the same 30 episodes would give, for TD(0), 0.36 0.54 · / 0.28 0.37 0.63 / 0.23 0.28 0.39, at most 0.11 from v_π, with A1 and B1 in order.
- **Monte Carlo, section 4: the control figure.** On-policy first-visit Monte Carlo control with ε = 0.1, values from 0, seed 28869, 30 episodes, 148 moves. Episode 1: 8 moves, A1 up, A2 right, B2 down, B1 right, C1 left, B1 right (random), C1 up, C2 up; its sweep gives returns 1, 0.9, (0.81 skipped: B1 right was first made at t = 3), 0.73, 0.66, 0.59, 0.53, 0.48, and the best moves then cut out the loop (in C1, up 0.9 beats left 0.73): 6 moves. Episode 2 averages Q(B1, right) to (0.66 + 0.81)/2 = 0.73. In episode 9 a random first move, right from A1, returns 0.73, above Q(A1, up) = 0.53, and the route becomes the 4-move A1 B1 C1 C2 C3 for good. At the end: 0.73, 0.79, 0.89, 1 along the route (the shortest trip's returns are 0.73, 0.81, 0.9, 1), Q(A1, up) = 0.54, and A3 and B3 never visited.
- **TD, section 1: the TD(0) figure.** TD(0) prediction for the random policy (each move ¼) on the cookie grid, γ = 0.9, α = 0.5, every V at 0, seed 15395, the same 30 episodes as the Monte Carlo prediction figure, 702 moves. Move 1 (A1 up) has δ = 0. Episode 1 takes 5 moves (A1 up, A2 left into the wall, A2 right, B2 right, C2 up), and only its last TD error is not 0: V(C2) 0 → 0.5. In episode 2, B2 → C2 targets 0.9 · 0.5 = 0.45 and V(B2) → 0.23; the next move, C2 → C1, has δ = −0.5 and V(C2) → 0.25. The route the estimates lead along is A1 A2 B2 C2 C3.
- **TD, section 5: the Sarsa figure.** One-step Sarsa on the cliff, α = 0.5, γ = 1, ε = 0.1, seed 557070, 30 episodes, 860 moves. Episode 1, played move by move: 14 moves. A1 down into the wall twice (A′ picks down again, so the same pair is updated twice, to −0.5 and then −0.75), A1 left into the wall, then the first fall, move 4, right from A1; A′ is chosen before the update, while right is still worth 0, so move 5 falls the same way (Q(A1, right): 0 → −50 → −75); then up, picked at random, and through row 3 to the goal (A2 B2 C2 C3 D3 E3 F3 F2 F1). The best moves first reach the goal in episode 9, in 9 moves through C3 and D3, and first run along the edge in episode 16; at move 722, in episode 24, the agent takes right from A2, picks down in B2 at random as A′ (worth −50.44, after one earlier fall), and Q(A2, right) drops from −5.8 to −28.62, below up (−5.88), for good.
